24. The fostering ritual, love and the 6th house {{t:01:40:36}}

How the speaker describes the ritual {{t:01:41:07}}

  • The child to be taken can be of any small age, two or five years, and sooner is better. The child is placed in the lap of both sets of parents. This is a "totka" (a folk trick). {{t:01:41:07}}
  • The foster parents say they want to bring up the child for life and could even say "we want to buy". They ask, "will you sell your child?" The birth parents say no. "Will you give him for upbringing?" Yes. They settle a sum and hand the child over. {{t:01:41:07}}
  • Then the foster parents ask whether the birth parents are happy with the price. Until they say yes, it is not done. He compares this to the power of words in a marriage vow ("I do", "qubool hai") and to the priest at Gaya asking whether the ancestors are pleased. {{t:01:41:40}}
  • Now the child has two sets of parents, and half the negative planets go to the other family. The birth family should be poor or already troubled. Do not give to a relative, who would blame you for any trouble later. {{t:01:42:13}}

I do not endorse this. A child must never be bought or sold, and any adoption must follow the law through the proper authorities. A "symbolic sale" does not change legal rights, and the speaker's idea that a child's troubles can be shifted to a poor family is his claim and is harmful. Do not attempt it.

The pairing of a "half affair" with being faithful and of a dog with romance are the speaker's own light remarks. The advice to keep an affair secret from a partner is his and I do not endorse deceiving a partner. Sweets for children are a fine charity, but check for allergies and diabetes first.

Pluto in the 6th: the enemy {{t:01:44:46}}

His lines about not fighting women or children are a folk saying and are not about any real group. Treat it as general advice to avoid conflict.

25. The Buddha's spit: the lamp does not ask who has come {{t:01:46:21}}

The story

A man spat on the Buddha. The Buddha asked, "anything else to say?" His disciple Ananda was puzzled. {{t:01:46:21}}

The meaning

The man was too upset to speak, so he spat. The next day he fell at the Buddha's feet. Again the Buddha asked, "anything more to say?" The man had no words left. {{t:01:46:52}}

The lesson

The Buddha said he loves without condition, like a lamp that gives light without asking who has come. That is Dharmaraj. If you have this mind, no enemy can kill you, and even a mad elephant could not kill the Buddha. {{t:01:47:24}}

  • To have this, drop everything and stay out of it, like Switzerland. Do not fret over what the child, brother or mother did. "As much as my quota with you, that much I am happy." He cites an old film, "Utsav", where the character says: you come to me, I am yours, you go to someone else, it is your karma. {{t:01:48:26}}
  • We instead fight daily: why did you stand there, who was looking at you. A beautiful wife's husband checks all the time who looks at her. {{t:01:48:57}}

The Buddha story is a parable the speaker retells in his own way. The remark on a husband who checks his wife is his humour and not advice to tolerate control.
