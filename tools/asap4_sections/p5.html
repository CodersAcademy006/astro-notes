12. Manifestation, "letting it be," and the story of Vashishtha {{t:02:10:42}}

The class closes with two audience questions tying the whole session's manifestation theme back to a well-known story from the Ramayana.

The question

If destiny is already written, why bother with manifestation at all, and does a chart's own placements decide how much a person can actually manifest? {{t:02:10:42}}

His answer

Manifestation, in his framework, does not override destiny; it refines or improves the outcome within what is already set, the same way a person can still choose how well they walk a path whose destination is fixed. {{t:02:11:13}}

His recommendation

Keep the mind calm enough to make good decisions along the way, rather than expecting manifestation to rewrite the outcome entirely. {{t:02:12:16}}

  • A story about the sage Vashishtha. After a personal tragedy, the sage is said to have tried and failed to end his own life by several means; reflecting on why he could not die, he realised his unborn grandchild still needed him to live, and chose to carry on, later finding peace by bowing to Ram's own humility rather than resenting how events had unfolded around a ritual timing he himself had set. {{t:02:13:50}}
  • His reading of Ram's exile despite a court astrologer's favourable timing for his coronation: Ram is held up as the model of accepting events without judging them as good or bad, which the speaker calls "leela" (divine play) rather than a failure of the astrologer's calculation. {{t:02:17:00}}
  • He distinguishes two kinds of "knowledge" in this closing discussion: consciously choosing an outcome (collapsing the wave into a particle, in his recurring quantum analogy), versus consciously allowing events to unfold without interference (staying in superposition, his term for the detached, "whatever happens, happens" stance); he treats both as equally valid forms of wisdom rather than ranking one above the other. {{t:02:18:33}}
  • He repeats his recurring claim that focused observation itself ("the electron knows it is being watched") is why detached awareness (sakshi bhav) changes outcomes, tying this back to the double-slit and Schrodinger's-cat analogies used in earlier classes in this series. {{t:02:20:38}}
  • As a cautionary example of chasing wealth without restraint, he describes a well-known Indian business family whose fortune was significantly reduced through internal disputes, without naming the family, and contrasts this with people who give generously and avoid hoarding. {{t:02:19:36}}
  • His closing practical takeaway: simply watching a reaction arise, without acting on it, is described as the real skill behind everything discussed in the class, illustrated by a child who, watched quietly without being scolded, tidies a room on their own. {{t:02:20:38}} He admits this quiet watching is far harder in practice than it sounds, since most people's bodies react before the mind can intervene. {{t:02:21:10}}

The retelling of the Vashishtha and Ram story, and the application of quantum-physics analogies to personal destiny, are the speaker's own spiritual framework, not verified history or physics. The anecdote about a business family's losses is presented without naming them and is the speaker's own characterisation, not a verified financial account.
