9. Donation rules: match the planet, match the place {{t:02:09:34}}

A student asks: there is a belief that a planet's effect in a house is weakened if you donate its items at a temple or gurudwara, especially for a planet that is already doing well there. The speaker calls this a widespread misunderstanding. {{t:02:09:34}}

  • A good (well-placed) planet's items are donated, but at a good place, which adds to your fortune rather than weakening it. A good planet's items are not given away in a needy or difficult context. {{t:02:09:34}}
  • A troubled (badly placed) planet's items are given in a needy context, not at a celebratory or sacred one. {{t:02:09:34}}

Two worked examples from the talk {{t:02:10:05}}

A well-placed Saturn in the 9th house

The more Saturn-ruled items you give (serving others, cleaning shoes at a gurudwara, giving away iron or kerosene), the more fortune grows. His example: a wealthy man stepping out of a Mercedes to personally clean shoes at a gurudwara, and prospering further for it, because he "activated" a planet that was already working for him. {{t:02:10:36}}

A well-placed Mars in the same slot

Give sweet, red items instead, such as halwa or gulab jamun, matching Mars's own colour and nature. {{t:02:11:06}}

A debilitated Saturn linked to the 12th house

Here the speaker gives a warning case, not a remedy: handing out Saturn's own items (shoes, iron) to strangers in this specific bad configuration is described as making things worse rather than better, bringing on headaches, body pain, domestic quarrels and hospital bills. A debilitated Jupiter paired with worshipping a guru's photo or a scripture in the wrong configuration gets the same warning. {{t:02:11:37}}

The speaker calls this topic "very deep" and says it deserves its own full class. These worked examples show that the same donation can help or harm depending on exactly how a planet sits in an individual chart, so this is not a safe thing to copy without checking your own chart first.

10. Gemstones: why this is not a simple "wear this stone" answer {{t:02:13:10}}

The question

If a retrograde planet is in, or linked to, the 6th, 8th or 12th house, should a gemstone be worn? The speaker's answer: not automatically, and retrograde motion is only one of several things that can cause the kind of affliction a gemstone might address. {{t:02:13:10}}

Why it gets complicated

The same sign can appear more than once as "active" across a chart, which changes where a stone should even be worn (which finger, or as a pendant). The stone's exact shade (he gives deep green versus light green emerald as an example) and even its country of origin (Zambian versus Colombian emerald) can matter for a specific chart. {{t:02:14:13}}

His caution about dosage

He compares this to a doctor prescribing an exact, specific dose: too little may do nothing, too much can actively harm. His own example is a case where a much higher weight of stone than advised was worn and, in his telling, badly backfired. He gives no stone weight as general advice here, only this warning. {{t:02:15:18}}

Nothing here should be read as a specific gemstone recommendation. The speaker himself says this requires chart-specific, in-person guidance, and this site's standing caution applies: decide any stone, its weight and its finger placement with a qualified astrologer who has read your full chart.

11. Rapid-fire questions {{t:02:07:59}}

How do you know which astrologer to trust? {{t:02:07:59}}

Do not announce your question first. Let the astrologer read the chart and tell you things unprompted; if they can do that convincingly, "wow" you, they have shown their skill. He applies the same test to choosing a guru: question them, test them, and once you run out of questions for someone, that is when they have earned the title. {{t:02:07:59}}

Can an AI like ChatGPT predict a horoscope as well as an astrologer? {{t:02:16:22}}

Both an astrologer and an AI work from data, so future versions may get much better at this. But the speaker separates intelligence from consciousness: he says consciousness evolves in living beings and cannot yet be "downloaded" into a machine, referencing science-fiction films about machine consciousness as a cultural touchpoint, not evidence. {{t:02:16:22}}

Does fate (prarabdh) work differently for someone of another religion? {{t:02:18:26}}

No. The body runs the same way regardless of religion or upbringing: the heart beats, the lungs work, and basic human expressions (a smile, anger) look the same across cultures, which he illustrates by saying dogs everywhere bark the same way. Cultural labels are, in his words, a layer people add on top, not a different underlying mechanism. {{t:02:18:26}}

This answer, and the aside about Sanskrit being an efficient language for computers to parse, are the speaker's own opinions on religion and language, not astrology.

Is it enough to just consider someone your guru, or do you need formal initiation? {{t:02:21:03}}

The speaker shares his own story: his chart, in his reading, gives him knowledge without needing a guru. Gurus he did approach wanted fees and an oath of secrecy, which he calls a business contract rather than a guru-disciple relationship, and he walked away. His advice: focus on becoming a good disciple first rather than rushing to anoint a guru; a genuine guru, in his view, does not take money for knowledge and will recognise a sincere student. {{t:02:21:35}}

What if someone gifts you an item tied to a planet that is negative in your chart? {{t:02:26:19}}

Pass it on rather than keeping it: give it to someone else, ideally to a saint or holy figure who will distribute it further. He describes this as a multiplying effect, the same logic behind a gurudwara's donation box. {{t:02:26:19}}

Is Ketu in the 12th house the same as Ketu in the 12th sign (Pisces)? {{t:02:27:51}}

No, and this is a common confusion. The fruit depends on which sign Ketu is actually sitting in, not simply which house number it occupies, because a real birth chart lets house and sign drift apart. Only in the idealised "Kaal Purush" chart (the cosmic man's chart used in texts like Lal Kitab) are house number and sign number locked together, with Ketu's own house fixed at the 12th. {{t:02:27:51}}
