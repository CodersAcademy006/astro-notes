4. Own house, friend's house, enemy's house: the rule behind every placement {{t:00:56:38}}

Before reading any planet sign by sign, the speaker lays out one rule he uses for all of them: a planet in its own sign is comfortable and gives its best result, in a friend's sign it can give an even better result than at home, and in the sign directly opposite its own (its enemy's house) it gives its worst result. {{t:00:56:38}}

Own house

Like being in your own home: the planet behaves well and gives a strong, reliable version of its nature. Example given: the Sun in Leo (its own sign) is at its best. {{t:00:57:10}}

Friend's house

Like a well-off in-law's house where you are pampered: can outperform even the planet's own sign. Example given: the Moon in Taurus, Venus's sign, which the speaker calls a near-unbeatable placement and compares to Krishna's chart. {{t:00:57:42}}

Enemy's house (the opposite sign)

The sign directly across the wheel from a planet's own sign is read as its weakest or most damaging placement, because two signs that face each other are natural rivals. Example given: Ram's chart has an exalted Saturn in the 4th house and an exalted Sun in the 10th house, two signs that face each other, which the speaker reads as Ram's single biggest chart weakness despite both planets being individually strong. {{t:00:50:52}}

Reading an opposite sign as automatically "worst" for every planet is the speaker's simplified teaching rule for this class, not the only factor classical Jyotish uses for strength.
