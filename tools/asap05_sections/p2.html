4. A science aside: atomic numbers and the outer planets {{t:00:37:35}}

A tangent the speaker builds on real atomic numbers, presented as a sign that "the cosmos is conscious" rather than as settled chemistry.

He bridges from the planet to the metal with an old-style glass thermometer: the shiny liquid inside it, which children sometimes broke open to play with, is the metal mercury itself, his starting point for the rest of this aside. {{t:00:33:56}}

  • The planet Mercury itself has almost no atmosphere and swings between extreme heat and cold next to the Sun, which the speaker reads as another sign of its "neuter" nature, belonging fully to neither warmth nor cold. {{t:00:29:11}}
  • He first explains an atomic number itself in simple terms, as the count of protons in an atom's nucleus (hydrogen has one, helium two, silver forty-seven), before giving mercury's and gold's numbers. {{t:00:38:08}}
  • Mercury (the metal, quicksilver) has atomic number 80, gold has atomic number 79, and the speaker tells a popular idea that removing one proton from mercury's nucleus could, in principle, turn it into gold. He frames this as a future possibility rather than a thing that has been done. A speculative, popularised claim about nuclear transmutation; presented as the speaker's own aside, not a chemistry fact to rely on. {{t:00:38:41}}
  • He also gives the metal's common name, quicksilver, as coming from how instantly it turns a bright silver colour the moment it touches real gold. {{t:01:48:30}}
  • A purified, solidified form of the metal is used to make a parad (mercury) shivling, an object some households keep; his only advice on it is to keep such an item simply in its place rather than worry over it. A brief description of a devotional object; not a safety or handling instruction. {{t:00:36:01}}
  • Mercury (the metal) and gold sit next to each other on the periodic table and, in his reading, "travel together" near the Sun in the chart, mirroring how close the planet Mercury always stays to the Sun. {{t:00:39:44}}
  • The outer planets line up the same way: Uranus pairs with the element uranium (atomic number 92), Neptune with neptunium (93), Pluto with plutonium (94), each named for its planet and each, in the speaker's view, matching that planet's character: Pluto/plutonium for death and the atomic bomb, Uranus/uranium for both destructive and constructive power (submarines, nuclear plants). {{t:00:40:45}}
  • His conclusion: "everything is conscious," and the universe's numbering shows up again in man-made chemistry, which he treats as more than coincidence. A personal, speculative interpretation of a numeric coincidence; not a scientific claim. {{t:00:41:16}}
  • He extends the same argument to Earth's atmosphere: oxygen sitting at roughly 21 percent, with life said to fail at either 19 or 23 percent, is offered as further evidence of deliberate, conscious design behind the solar system rather than accident. A personal, non-scientific argument for intelligent design; his own belief, not a scientific claim. {{t:01:12:02}}
  • He correctly notes that most of that oxygen actually comes from the ocean rather than from land plants. {{t:01:11:30}}

He adds that Pluto is the planet of hidden death and the underworld "mafia," and ties the planet to the weapons trade as the world's single most profitable industry, and Neptune to the entertainment industry, since both run on illusion and spectacle built to draw attention. General commentary on industries; his own framing, not a factual claim about any specific country or company. {{t:00:30:48}}

Under Neptune specifically he places big-budget war dramas, extraterrestrial and space-themed films, perfume and luxury branding, calling all of it manufactured spectacle rather than substance, whatever its production value. A general opinion about entertainment and luxury industries; his own framing. {{t:01:31:52}}

  • He calls underworld and organised-crime dealings, which he again files under Pluto, the single largest money game on the planet, well ahead of any legitimate industry. A speculative, unverifiable claim about illegal economies; his own framing. {{t:01:30:16}}
  • His rough wealth hierarchy by planet: Mars holds the biggest land bank, so land work also tends to make people wealthy; Venus governs luxury, fashion and show; and under Mercury sit the go-between professions, trade, commission work and deal-making. A general, speculative ranking of industries by planet; his own framing. {{t:01:32:55}}
  • Jupiter, by contrast, is given almost no money of its own in this scheme, "wisdom is poor," since its reward is knowledge and guidance rather than wealth or indulgence. {{t:01:33:29}}

5. The balance scale: Mercury and Venus {{t:00:41:48}}

The speaker's central image for the rest of the class: life runs on two pans of one scale, Mercury (intellect, trade, the capacity to earn) on one side and Venus (comfort, enjoyment, the capacity to enjoy what is earned) on the other. Tip one pan too far and the other falls.

Problem

A huge house that is never actually lived in, constant travel so the owner never enjoys their own comfort, or a very intelligent person who ends up working to make someone else rich rather than enjoying their own skill. {{t:00:42:18}}

Cause

Mercury (earning, know-how) has run far ahead of Venus (the capacity to actually enjoy what is earned), or the reverse: comfort without the sense to manage it. {{t:00:43:19}}

The speaker's framing

Learn to feel satisfied with what you actually experience rather than what you accumulate. In his words, wealth taken into the next life is "nothing, seven is all they say you carry," so the measure that matters is how much of what you have was actually enjoyed. His own view on satisfaction and wealth; not financial advice. {{t:00:43:50}}

Girls, boys and an unsettled Mercury {{t:00:43:50}}

  • The speaker describes some young women (and men) as staying stuck in a child-like, assertive speech pattern ("I will eat this, I will go there") well past childhood, which he reads as an unsettled, over-active Mercury that has not let a woman settle into her Venus (relational, receptive) side. The speaker's own generalisation about gendered speech and behaviour; a personal view, not a factual claim about any individual. {{t:00:44:22}}
  • He is explicit that this is not ordinary tomboyish behaviour in a child; the pattern he means specifically shows up as a loss of felt enjoyment (bhog) once a woman is grown, not as anything wrong with the child itself. The speaker's own generalisation; a personal view, not a factual claim. {{t:00:45:24}}
  • His claimed consequence: when Mercury stays this assertive, Venus (marriage, intimacy, enjoyment) is said to suffer, leaving a person with achievements, money and relationships but no felt enjoyment (bhog) of any of them. A personal claim linking speech patterns to marriage and enjoyment; not a factual or medical claim. {{t:00:45:54}}
  • His phone analogy for the two planets: an old keypad phone, needing several presses to spell one letter, is Mercury's effortful, logical style; a modern touchscreen, responding to a single touch, is Venus's effortless style. {{t:01:01:36}}
