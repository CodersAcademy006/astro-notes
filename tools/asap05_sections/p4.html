10. Retrograde Mercury, threads and other specific remedies {{t:01:36:34}}

Problem

Retrograde (vakri) Mercury, wherever it sits, is called the single biggest Mercury fault: sharp, bitter speech, constant arguing, and a person who can "drive others to the edge" through sheer verbal persistence. {{t:01:36:34}}

Why

A retrograde Mercury is, in the speaker's description, trying to move against the Sun's pull while still bound to it, a constant internal friction that shows up as restlessness in speech. {{t:01:18:49}}

Fix

An emerald is the main remedy for retrograde Mercury in almost any sign; if Mercury sits specifically in the 4th or 12th house and is also retrograde, he instead recommends wearing it on the ring finger (associated with the Sun) rather than the little finger. A full chart reading should confirm which finger and how many carats before wearing any stone. Gemstone advice is the speaker's own practice, not medical, legal or financial advice. {{t:01:19:22}}

He also cautions against reaching for an emerald blindly: if the 3rd sign (Gemini) is not actually present in that house, other remedies should be tried first and the stone considered only afterward. {{t:01:19:52}}

Not every retrograde Mercury is treated as purely negative: if it is not badly placed but simply sits in the 4th or 5th house, even in a weak sign, the speaker credits it with an unusual gift for astrophysics, invention and mathematical thinking, even while speech itself still turns sharper. A specific claim linking a chart placement to scientific aptitude; the speaker's own framework, not a factual claim. {{t:01:21:56}}

  • Mother-daughter friction, or trouble keeping female friends, is blamed on a disturbed Mercury placed with the Moon or in the 4th house; the remedy given is a Moon remedy rather than anything aimed at Mercury directly. {{t:01:36:34}}
  • Mercury is rarely used for coloured-thread remedies on its own; green thread is reserved mainly for the 6th and 8th houses, where it is said to protect a related house (commonly the 2nd) as a side effect. {{t:01:36:03}}
  • "Green water": fill a glass or white bottle with water, wrap green paper or cloth around it, leave it in sunlight for about an hour, and drink it for nerve, dental, intestinal and joint-related discomfort the speaker attributes to Mercury. He is explicit that ordinary medical treatment should still be sought if the problem is serious. A traditional home remedy from the speaker; not medical advice, and no medicine or dosing is implied here. {{t:01:50:35}}
  • For exam days specifically: the speaker advises against drinking plain water from the time of waking until the exam ends (lime water, tea or coffee are allowed instead), avoiding white or very plain clothing, and keeping a small photograph wrapped in green cloth at home if Mercury's chart placement calls for it. He treats this as a Moon-related exam-nerves fix, corrected "through Mercury." The speaker's own exam-day ritual; not medical or academic advice. {{t:01:49:01}}

11. Questions from the class {{t:01:54:14}}

Is intellect (buddhi) the same as wisdom (prajna)? No, in the speaker's framework: buddhi (Mercury) is logical, "circumstantial," and belongs to the physical, sensory world; prajna is an "energy body" matter, closer to grace or love, and the speaker says it cannot really be demonstrated the way logic can, only lived. He adds that a person can have deep wisdom without the ability to express it clearly (a Mercury gap), and, more rarely, a very articulate Mercury with little underlying wisdom to express. The speaker's own philosophical distinction; presented as his personal view. {{t:01:54:44}}

He goes further on love itself: in his definition, real love cannot exist between two living people at all, only attachment, desire or possessiveness can; love in his sense belongs only to the dead or to the divine, since a living relationship always carries some demand attached to it. A strong, personal definition of love; the speaker's own philosophical opinion, not a factual claim. {{t:01:55:16}}

My father is alive and performed my shraddh (ancestral rite) on my behalf when a priest refused to let me do it myself. Is my pitra dosh (ancestral affliction) resolved, or do I need to do it again? The speaker says the father's own rite is resolved by what the father did, but the asker will still need to perform his own eventually; he calls the old rule "your father is alive, so you cannot do it" an outdated social convention from a time when elders controlled the household's children, money and women tightly, rather than something to take literally today. He frames waiting for a parent's death before fixing a chart fault as unreasonable in an age when people commonly live into their eighties, and suggests doing the rite quietly ("the way you did your love marriage quietly") rather than making it a public argument with the family. The speaker's own view on family ritual and tradition; a personal opinion on religious practice, not a ruling of any tradition. {{t:01:57:20}}

He links the modern rise in this kind of question to wider public awareness of developmental and genetic conditions: where earlier generations had no name for a child's differences, he says families now recognise terms for specific conditions and often trace the cause back to an unresolved ancestral (pitra) issue in the chart. A claim linking developmental conditions to ancestral chart faults; the speaker's own belief, not a medical or genetic explanation, and not medical advice. {{t:01:59:55}}

If the 7th house is badly afflicted, does a second marriage work? The speaker's answer is blunt: it depends entirely on which planet is doing the damage. A placement he associates with Neptune-like deception can survive any number of marriages; a placement he associates with Pluto-like destruction cannot be fixed by remarriage at all and, in his account, pushes the person toward multiple relationships regardless of what anyone advises. He is explicit that a person does not have unlimited free choice here ("we can't just decide to be good, try it and see"), a fairly fatalistic claim about marriage and relationships. A strong, deterministic claim about marriage and personal choice; the speaker's own view, not a claim to be relied on for any relationship or legal decision. {{t:02:00:26}}

His wider point is that most major life choices, marriage, having children, where to live, what to study, feel far less open to free choice than people assume; he suggests making peace with this and finding contentment within it rather than chasing an unconstrained "my own way" life. A fatalistic personal philosophy about free will and life choices; the speaker's own opinion. {{t:02:00:58}}

Must an emerald be worn whenever Mercury sits in the 6th or 12th house, or only sometimes? Yes, he says, regardless of the house, as long as the sign itself calls for it; the emerald treats the sign's own condition, so if the house placement is the actual problem rather than the sign, a different planet's remedy may be needed alongside it rather than instead of it. Gemstone advice is the speaker's own practice; not medical, legal or financial advice. {{t:01:53:12}}

What happens with Mercury specifically in the 12th house and the relationship with the father? He adds that when the 9th house's planet is "exhausted," it effectively "releases" the person from that bind. A strong, deterministic claim linking wealth to a family relationship and a specific chart placement; the speaker's own predictive framework, not a factual or financial claim. {{t:01:53:42}}

Asked whether Brahma, Vishnu and Mahesh are themselves forms given to a deeper, formless reality, the speaker agrees they are forms we have given names and shapes to, and says that as science progresses (he mentions multidimensional and string theory ideas by name only, not in technical depth), more of what was once called "divine punishment" (a deity's anger causing illness, for instance) gets explained by ordinary causes instead.
