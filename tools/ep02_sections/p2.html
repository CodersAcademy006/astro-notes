6. How the chart works: lagna, degrees and houses {{t:00:28:40}}

The speaker spends a long stretch on one technical point. A planet that looks as if it sits in a house may not be working there. You need the degrees, not just the sign. {{t:00:31:52}} {{t:00:34:01}}

  • 12 lagnas in a day. The sky is a 360 degree wheel cut into 12 signs of 30 degrees. As the earth turns, a new lagna (rising sign) takes the first house about every two hours. It varies a little because the earth does not move in a perfect circle but an oval, like an egg. {{t:00:04:12}} {{t:00:28:40}} {{t:00:29:13}}
  • The day passes from morning (east), through noon to evening and midnight. Morning, noon, evening and midnight fall at the four main points of the chart. {{t:00:29:13}} {{t:00:29:43}}
  • Lagna degree. Someone born at 7:00 in a lagna that runs from 6 to 8 has the lagna at about 15 degrees. {{t:00:30:17}} {{t:00:31:22}}

The Mars example {{t:00:30:50}}
