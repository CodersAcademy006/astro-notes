10. Household Ganesh: placement rules and the two-idol warning {{t:01:47:17}}

  • He recommends a northeast placement in the home, never at the main entrance and never facing outward from it, calling an entrance placement highly negative. {{t:01:49:51}}
  • He objects to Ganesh used as a decorative motif: on a doorstep welcome mat, on silver coins handed out casually, or on piggy banks, calling all of these disrespectful of the deity's actual purpose. {{t:00:34:02}}
  • Once a Ganesh idol or photo is no longer wanted, he says it should be set aside respectfully (not discarded carelessly) after a final acknowledgment, rather than simply thrown out. {{t:01:48:50}}
  • A photo of Ganesh together with Lakshmi is counted as holding two deities at once for the purposes of this two-idol rule, not one. {{t:01:47:17}}
  • Ganesh Visarjan (immersion). He reads the Mumbai-wide practice of immersing the clay Ganesh idol in water at the end of the festival as a deliberate lesson: a human-made god, built from earth, is deliberately returned to the earth, which he calls proof that people can let go of even their own creations of the divine. {{t:01:38:22}}

His strongest specific claim here: keeping two Ganesh idols in the house, he says, increases the risk of either spouse having an affair. He calls this claim sensitive enough that he expects backlash for saying it. These notes record it because he said it, do not endorse or verify it, and do not treat it as relationship advice. {{t:01:47:50}}
