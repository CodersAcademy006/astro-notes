6. The Moon with other planets {{t:01:00:54}}

Before turning to houses, the speaker runs through what happens when the Moon sits together with each other planet, treating the Moon as the one stable "water" element reacting differently depending on company. He flags the Moon as most dangerous when it falls in the 1st or 8th sign generally. {{t:01:00:54}} He frames this as a quick, generalised sense of each pairing rather than a full reading. {{t:01:01:26}}

Moon withHis reading
SunCombust and invisible: the Moon's own identity disappears next to the Sun. {{t:01:01:57}}
RahuEclipsed: Rahu is read as swallowing the Moon the way it swallows the Sun in a solar eclipse. {{t:01:01:57}}
Varuna (the sea, in his outer-planet scheme)Friendly: both are water, so this is read as an easy, compatible placement. {{t:01:02:29}}
JupiterCalm and affectionate; loosely tied to the "Gajakesari yoga" reputation, though he downplays how much weight that specific yoga actually carries. {{t:01:03:00}}
SaturnRead as liquid turning toward intoxicants (he names alcohol specifically); the Moon's own steady, cooling nature is said to be overridden. {{t:01:03:31}}
PlutoThe Moon's water gets trapped and dried, paralleling Pluto's mythic role as the force that ends a life. {{t:01:04:01}}
KetuWater trapped in clouds: the mind gets stuck, prone to overthinking. {{t:01:04:33}}
MarsFire and water in direct conflict: read as "maha dangerous," especially reinforcing the general warning about the Moon in the 1st or 8th sign. {{t:01:04:33}}
VenusPleasant, like perfume: a comfortable, enjoyable combination. {{t:01:05:36}}
MercuryOpen sky: vast and directionless, since Mercury itself is read as having "no fixed character." {{t:01:06:08}}

These planet-pair readings, including the claim connecting a Saturn-Moon combination to alcohol, are the speaker's personal framework and are not a factual or medical claim about anyone's habits.

7. The Moon as mother: house by house {{t:01:06:38}}

The speaker then switches from sign-pairings to the Moon's actual house (bhava) placement, read through its role as "mother" in the chart. He moves between "sign number" and "house" somewhat loosely in his own speech; the table below keeps only the house-specific readings he gives clearly. He does not give a separate, clear reading for the 4th or 8th house in this part (the 4th was covered earlier through direction and the home, and he repeats that the 1st and 8th signs generally carry the most risk). {{t:01:06:38}}

HouseHis reading
1stA commanding, even domineering mother (or, for a woman native, a commanding husband); he adds that a mother should generally not hold this "commanding" role, or it brings its own problems. {{t:01:06:38}}
2ndA family-devoted mother; household staff and workers are treated warmly, "like family," and feel genuinely cared for. {{t:01:07:40}}
3rdRestless and dual-minded (he links this to Gemini): such a person repeats the same point several times, needing it confirmed back to them before they feel settled, whether they are the native or their mother. {{t:01:08:11}}
5thOvershadowed by the Sun's placement there: the person feels habitually suppressed and prefers to let someone else speak or decide on their behalf. {{t:01:09:13}}
6thRead as Rahu's sign and genuinely difficult: the mother becomes a kind of adversary, or the person becomes a "mother figure" to everyone else while their own relationship with their actual mother stays strained; he also describes a "Kaikeyi yoga" here, where an elder woman's interference stirs ongoing domestic conflict and can push a married couple toward separation. {{t:01:09:45}}
7thA strong, good placement if well aspected: the mind stays fixed on the spouse, and (if well placed) the spouse stays devoted for life; if poorly placed, he claims a wife may end up treating her husband like a child rather than a partner. {{t:01:12:54}}
9thVery strong if the person treats their mother with god-like devotion: strong ties to religion and whichever faith they were raised in. {{t:01:14:29}}
10thExcellent if household staff and employees are treated with maternal warmth, producing outstanding work; however, if the sign involved is Saturn's, the work itself proceeds very slowly. {{t:01:15:32}}
11thA "trapped" Moon, read as caught near Pluto's domain: if the person channels this into duty-bound (dharmic) work, the Moon eventually lets go and life feels easier; otherwise, it stays trapped. See the next section for the risk he attaches to this. {{t:01:17:05}}
12thDoubt, suspicion and a persistent feeling of being cheated or unseen by the world; read as Ketu's domain, water trapped in clouds. {{t:01:19:40}}
  • His own aside on method: a single placement rarely tells the whole story, since planets interact across degrees and the rest of the chart; he compares reading one placement in isolation to assuming two family members are fighting just because they are both somewhere in the same house, when in fact they might be sitting in separate rooms. {{t:01:28:28}} A gap of 20 degrees or more between two planets in the same sign, he adds, can mean they barely interact at all. {{t:01:28:58}}
  • He repeats, as a teaching caution, that these house-by-house readings are generalised starting points rather than a complete reading: a full consultation always requires the whole chart, including degrees and nakshatra, not one placement taken alone. {{t:01:29:59}}

This entire house-by-house reading of the Moon as "mother," including the "Kaikeyi yoga" and the claims about wives treating husbands as children, is the speaker's personal astrological framework. These notes do not endorse it as fact about anyone's real marriage or family.
