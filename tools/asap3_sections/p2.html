4. Protective and opposing deities: how the chart decides them {{t:00:21:59}}

Branching off a question about a real birth chart, the speaker explains how a person's 9th-house sign is read as assigning both a protective deity (Ishta) and an opposing one, and argues this works whether or not the person actively practises any religion. {{t:00:21:59}}

The claim

Whatever sign falls in the 9th house decides which deity is protective; certain other signs in the same chart are read as the "negativist" (opposing) energy for that same person. {{t:00:21:59}}

His worked example

In one example chart, three different placements were read as hostile to three different deities at once, which he reads as making that person naturally solitary for life even without choosing it, and as weakening that person's 9th house (broadly: fortune and the father) regardless of whether they worship any of those deities. {{t:00:22:30}}

His point

The underlying temperament is automatic and chart-driven; ritual or its absence does not change which energies are already active for that person. {{t:00:24:03}}

  • Public figures as chart illustrations. The speaker uses several unnamed contemporary public figures as examples in this section: a well-known political leader who habitually addresses crowds as "friends" (read as a strong fourth-house Mars), a prominent entrepreneur in the space industry who has said he follows no religion (read as weak 9th-house Jupiter, which the speaker claims would be disturbed rather than helped by conventional worship), and a major film actor who follows a different faith tradition from the Hindu framework being taught. These are the speaker's own chart interpretations of real public figures, not confirmed biographical facts, and these notes do not name them or repeat the specific claims made about their real lives. {{t:00:25:07}}
  • His general point from these examples: a person's outward behaviour and success pattern, in his view, tracks their chart's planetary placements directly, regardless of which religion (or no religion) they outwardly practise. {{t:00:26:42}}
  • He extends this to an unnamed well-known entertainer who follows a different faith tradition, arguing that the same planetary mechanics apply "whichever god" a person prays to, since the chart's underlying structure, not the specific religious label, is what is doing the work in his framework. {{t:00:26:42}} The specific deity named in prayer, in his view, changes nothing about which planet is actually doing the work. {{t:00:27:14}}
  • He gives a separate, generic example of a weak Venus sitting in an unfavourable house: in his claim this plays out as constant marital friction and prolonged unemployment, "automatically," as the person goes about ordinary life. {{t:00:27:14}} A weak Saturn, he adds, shows the same automatic pattern in how a person treats employees or staff. {{t:00:27:14}}

5. Signs your Moon is disturbed, and the general fix {{t:00:31:28}}

Signs at home

Water leakage or seepage anywhere in the house: a dripping tap, a damp patch, standing water. He treats this as a direct, practical sign of a disturbed Moon, separate from any chart reading. {{t:00:31:59}}

What it does

A disturbed Moon is said to show up as a foggy mind and a dulled sense of romance, playfulness and simple enjoyment. {{t:00:31:59}}

The general fix

Repair leaks immediately, and after sunset stop eating the three "white" things: rice, milk and plain water (detailed below). {{t:00:46:41}}

  • Immunity and who gets hit hardest. He claims the Moon governs the body's immune defences (white blood cells, the lymphatic system), and that during a shared illness in a household, whoever has the weakest Moon placement in their own chart is affected first and worst, largely because of their mental state rather than pure biology. He cites the Covid period as his example. {{t:00:32:31}} He contrasts this with people who stayed visibly unworried through the same period and, in his telling, came through it untouched. {{t:00:33:01}}
  • He links a disturbed Moon to repetitive, perfectionist behaviour: compulsively rearranging things, repeated handwashing, an inability to let a small disorder go. {{t:00:37:46}} People around a Moon-afflicted perfectionist, he adds, rarely get a moment's peace either. {{t:00:38:17}}
  • Comparison and "fermentation." He claims people who constantly compare themselves to others (feeling cheated by fate, resentful of what others have) develop excess internal yeast activity, to the point that their body effectively produces its own trace ethanol through fermentation in digestion, independent of whether they actually drink alcohol; he contrasts this with the smaller share of liver disease that doctors attribute to people who do drink. {{t:00:40:25}}
  • Oversensitivity is treated as another Moon symptom: taking any criticism badly, either lashing out or dissolving into tears, and being unable to let an insult go. Skin allergies, dust and cough-cold type reactions are grouped under the Moon too, while skin conditions specifically are attributed instead to Ketu. {{t:00:41:57}} Even a cucumber, which he notes is almost entirely water, can trigger an allergic reaction in this reading. {{t:00:44:02}}
  • Children who are described as constantly irritable, or whose school performance and clarity of speech are poor, are read as having a disturbed Moon; he suggests changing a troubled child's effective "name" (covered in the name remedy section below) as one fix. {{t:00:44:34}} Unclear or flat speech in Hindi specifically, he adds, is another marker of the same affliction. {{t:00:45:05}}
  • Taking a false oath "on my mother" is treated as something that directly dries out a person's own water element; his suggestion is simply to stop swearing oaths on the mother at all rather than risk a false one. {{t:00:45:05}} If an oath is needed at all, he suggests swearing on anything other than the mother. {{t:00:45:36}}
  • Reports of being haunted or of "hallucinations" (sensing someone behind a curtain) are read as the mind fixating on one idea until it becomes obsessive; he is agnostic on whether ghosts are real ("that's for the Pluto class"), and says that practices like waving a peacock feather to clear the sensation work by activating Ketu, not by causing any harm. {{t:00:45:36}}
  • The sunset rule in detail. After sunset, avoid rice, milk and plain water in their pure white form: tint rice with turmeric or make it into a pulao, flavour milk as tea, coffee or a sweetened drink, and add tulsi, cardamom, saffron or fennel to water, or drink it from a copper, brass or clay vessel. He frames the Jain custom of not eating after sunset as resting on the same underlying logic. People with a strong Moon placement, he adds, can ignore this rule without consequence; the rule is aimed at those whose Moon is already weak. {{t:00:46:41}}
  • For children sitting exams with handwriting trouble (read as a weak Moon, since speaking is governed by Mercury but writing by the Moon in his scheme), he suggests withholding white clothing (including undergarments) from sunset the evening before through the exam, sending water tinted with a tulsi leaf instead, and drinking water from a green bottle to help with hallucination-type distraction. {{t:00:49:51}}
  • Mercury is described as "the Moon's child," so keeping Mercury's own matters in good order (never taking money from a sister, aunt or other close woman relative even by mistake, and treating transgender people with respect and generosity rather than bargaining down what they ask for) is offered as an indirect way of protecting the Moon too. {{t:00:56:05}}
  • The gemstone remedy he gives for the Moon generally is pearl (moti). {{t:00:57:09}}
