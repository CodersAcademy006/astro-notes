10. Mannat: a vow is a deal, not a wish {{t:01:29:37}}

  • He frames a mannat (a vow made to a deity) as a negotiation, comparable to talking a traffic officer down from a fine: approach with genuine humility and full-hearted commitment, not demands. {{t:01:29:37}}
  • His claim: how fully a vow is kept, not which specific deity receives it, decides whether it is granted; any ishta, however obscure or informal, can work if approached with complete sincerity. {{t:01:31:41}}
  • He separately warns that the underlying want (greed, ego, envy) still gets "paid for" somewhere, in some bodily or mental form, even when the vow itself succeeds, so a vow made from greed carries a cost regardless of outcome. {{t:01:28:03}}

He singles out jealousy (eersha) as the most dangerous of these underlying motives, claiming it specifically damages Varuna (the deity of the chart's western, all-seeing point) and shows up as uncontrolled blood pressure in people who constantly complain. These notes record this as his own claim and give no medical advice about blood pressure. {{t:01:28:36}}
