6. Chitragupt and the chemistry of anger {{t:00:31:52}}

He returns to the Chitragupt idea (the mythological recorder reframed as the brain's visual cortex) to explain why anger seems to strike for no clear reason. {{t:00:31:52}}

  • His claim: the visual cortex, responsible for about 90 percent of brain activity in his account, silently photographs everything present during a moment of anger, not just the person who caused it, down to background details like the weather or passing animals. {{t:00:32:54}}
  • Any later recurrence of those same background details, in his telling, can trigger anger again with no apparent cause, because the brain's stored "photograph" fires without the person consciously remembering why. {{t:00:33:26}}
  • His claimed physical cost of anger. He describes anger as a form of internal fire that, sustained over years, dries out the body's water content and leads in sequence to rheumatism, then arthritis, then osteoporosis. {{t:00:33:58}}

This progression from chronic anger to rheumatism, arthritis and osteoporosis is the speaker's own claim and is not a verified medical mechanism. These notes record it as his claim, do not endorse it, and give no medical advice; any joint or bone condition should be assessed by a qualified doctor. {{t:00:33:58}}
