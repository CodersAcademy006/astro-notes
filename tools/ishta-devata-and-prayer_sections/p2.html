4. The 12 percent ceiling, again {{t:00:22:47}}

He repeats a claim from his other classes: a fixed share of anyone's life, which he rounds to 12 percent, cannot be changed by any amount of ritual, effort or prayer, whether performed by an ordinary person or, in his telling, by great sages. {{t:00:23:34}}

The problem

People spend years fighting a fixed outcome: a failed marriage, a child's path, an illness, calling the eventual resolution "the fruit of hard work" when the fixed period simply ran its course. {{t:00:23:34}}

Why it happens

He treats this ceiling as non-negotiable even for figures he considers legendary within his own tradition, Arjun's war or a sage's own trial, to argue no one is exempt. {{t:00:23:34}}

His suggested fix

A good ishta and a strong bhagya can soften the setting (he gives moving a sick person to an air-conditioned room as his example) without changing that a fixed outcome must still be lived through. {{t:00:24:06}}
