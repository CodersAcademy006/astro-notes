11. Story: Bokoju and the broken statue {{t:01:52:29}}

A Zen-style story told to show what taking grace means. {{t:01:52:29}}

  • In the old feudal days kings declared themselves gods and paid priests to say so, and religion was used to crush people. He says false religion is made by Ketu, Rahu and Neptune, the illusion-makers. {{t:01:52:29}}
  • A tiny orphan boy, Bokoju, is brought to a large monastery of a great master. He is too small even for a broom, so a small broom and a small mop are made for him. He serves and learns. {{t:01:53:31}}
  • Meditation (dhyana) means doing nothing, not even kirtan. He says our tradition began so, but we drifted into drums and noise. {{t:01:54:02}}
  • He sees the master bow every day before one small Buddha statue, passed down for 100 years from the master's teachers. The boy cannot see it from his height, and asks a junior teacher to lift him. {{t:01:54:33}}
  • The monks tell him nobody may touch it. The master himself cleans it. All the boy wants is the master's love, and he plans all night to clean it himself. {{t:01:55:35}}
  • In the morning he stacks two stools, climbs, hangs from the shelf, and falls with the statue. It breaks. He decides he should die and goes to ask the master's leave. {{t:01:56:07}}
  • The master comes, gathers the pieces, and tells him to step back because the small shards could cut his hands. The boy says "you do not understand, I broke the guru of gurus' statue." The master says "I understand, son. It was only for up to today. The hundred years are complete." {{t:01:57:10}}
  • That day Bokoju gained full self-knowledge: even the most valuable thing finishes one day, and we must learn to let go at that moment. He became a great saint. {{t:01:58:44}}

The lesson

We cannot drop old memories, old hatred, old days. That is where grace begins when we do. The sea takes revenge on no one. {{t:01:58:44}}

Practice

When someone hands you dirt, accept it and throw it into the cosmos. Neptune switches on. {{t:01:59:16}}

Why it blocks

We punish ourselves and others for what has gone by. Drop that attitude and Neptune is pleased. {{t:02:00:20}}

  • God lifts the best athlete the way a coach picks the best runner for the Olympics. He lifts the child who says "forget it, move on", who forgives and forgets, and whose navel centre is strong, then gives witness mind and opens the "akashic portals". {{t:01:59:48}}
  • At the heart centre you can manifest anything when you no longer have the wish itself. {{t:02:00:20}}
