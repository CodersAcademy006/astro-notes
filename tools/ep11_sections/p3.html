5. Why a failed exam can be the cheaper outcome {{t:00:36:38}}

The speaker's most direct claim in this class: every placement "takes something" from a chart, and it is far better to let it take a fee, a year, or a failed attempt than to let it take the body's health instead. {{t:00:37:41}} {{t:00:38:45}}

His example

A debilitated Mars tied to both the 4th and 5th houses is said to guarantee some kind of surgery, a bad knee or back, ovarian cysts or a caesarean for a girl, unless something else absorbs that "bill" first. {{t:00:37:41}} {{t:00:38:12}}

His reasoning

He treats a placement like this as a debt that gets collected one way or another; a failed exam, a wasted course fee or a lost admission deposit can be that debt's cheaper form, paid in money and time rather than in the body. {{t:00:38:12}} {{t:00:38:45}}

His advice

Do not chase a refund on a lost admission fee, and do not treat a failed attempt as wasted. If no such setback is on the cards, generous giving (he gives a gurdwara gold donation as his own example) is offered as an alternative payment. {{t:00:39:45}} {{t:00:40:16}}

  • A disturbed Saturn is said to almost guarantee that a child takes an admission somewhere they never actually complete (a language course is his example); he says to let that happen rather than call the child lazy or irresponsible. {{t:00:37:09}} {{t:00:37:41}}
  • If a child's studies are not helped by any of the planets above, he reads it instead as missing ancestors' grace, and ties it to how older gurukul-style education deliberately taught children to remember their ancestors early in life. {{t:00:40:47}} {{t:00:41:18}}
  • He also frames giving generously, rather than reluctantly, as part of the same payment: a grudging gift is treated as a smaller, less effective sacrifice than one given freely. {{t:00:39:15}} {{t:00:40:16}}

Treating a medical outcome such as a caesarean or ovarian cysts as something a financial "sacrifice" can substitute for is the speaker's own belief, not medical fact. A pregnancy or gynaecological concern needs a doctor, not a donation in place of one.

6. Picking the deity that actually matches your chart {{t:00:41:18}}

Telling every child to simply "remember Saraswati" before an exam is, in the speaker's account, often aiming at the wrong deity altogether, since the sign actually sitting in that child's 9th house may belong to someone else entirely. {{t:00:41:18}} {{t:00:41:49}}

The mismatch

A child is told to invoke Saraswati, tied here to Rahu's sign, when their own chart's relevant placement actually belongs to Durga (Mercury's sign) or Ganesh (Ketu's sign); invoking the wrong one is said to hurt the exam rather than help it. {{t:00:41:49}} {{t:00:42:22}}

His analogy

Paracetamol and Disprin are both good medicines, but only one is right for a given problem; picking a deity works the same way; he also compares it to asking the parent who can actually grant permission rather than the one who cannot. {{t:00:42:22}} {{t:00:42:52}}

Remedy

Match the remedy to the sign that is actually placed in the relevant house for that specific child, rather than defaulting to the most commonly named deity. {{t:00:41:49}}

  • If Mars is debilitated on the study placement, giving the child something sweet right before they leave is said to make them miss questions that were visible all along. {{t:00:42:52}}
  • If both the Moon and Saturn are weak on signs 1 and 8, avoiding dairy, plain water and other white foods before leaving is his specific caution; plain wheat flour (atta) is not counted in that "white" category. {{t:00:43:22}} {{t:00:44:56}}
  • He is explicit that none of this depends on which religion someone follows: the planets are said to affect everyone's chart the same way regardless of personal faith. {{t:00:44:56}} {{t:00:45:27}}
  • A debilitated Sun on certain signs calls for avoiding salty food and orange-coloured items before an exam, since he ties this placement to clarity of mind and sight on the day. {{t:00:45:57}} {{t:00:46:01}}
  • A sign ruled by Mercury on this placement calls for black tea, without milk or sugar, which he says sets Rahu and Mercury together favourably for the day. {{t:00:46:32}} {{t:00:47:02}}
