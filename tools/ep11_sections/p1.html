1. A leftover topic: the sign 5 in the 9th house, and what not to donate there {{t:00:00:36}}

The class opens by finishing a point left over from the donation and charity class: what happens when sign 5, the Sun's own sign, sits in the 9th house (the place of worship), and which items to avoid giving there depending on which planet's sign actually sits in that house. {{t:00:00:36}} {{t:00:01:41}}

His rule

Whatever planet is unfriendly to the sign sitting in your 9th house, giving that planet's item at your place of worship is said to backfire, sometimes badly. {{t:00:02:12}} {{t:00:03:16}}

His examples

With Sun's own sign there, Saturn's items (oil, iron) turn negative and Pluto (his name for a death-linked placement) brings repeated thoughts of death. With a Moon-debilitated sign there, giving water or opening a free water stand ("pyau") is said to ruin the giver rather than help anyone. {{t:00:02:12}} {{t:00:03:46}}

Safe gifts, by his list

Wheat or gold for the Sun's friends; red sweets (never black ones) for Mars; green items or cash for Mercury; yellow besan or laddoos for Jupiter. Ghee, curd, new clothes and anything "new and nice" are kept away from Venus's placement there, and incense or lamp smoke is avoided altogether at a place of worship. {{t:00:02:43}} {{t:00:04:47}} {{t:00:06:20}}

  • He adds that begging or complaining out loud at a place of worship, rather than simply stating a wish calmly, is itself read as weakening the Sun's placement there. {{t:00:03:16}}
  • A gift meant for a guru, such as a blanket, should be a genuinely useful size rather than a token one, since a too-small gift is treated as poor etiquette rather than a real offering. {{t:00:06:20}} {{t:00:06:52}}

The claim that silently resenting a request at a temple, or privately wishing death on someone, measurably damages your own fortune is the speaker's own religious framework, not a verified claim.

2. A quick sketch of the vastu deities, and why most people cannot remember them {{t:00:07:23}}

The speaker then gives a brief, simplified tour of the home vastu grid: roughly three dozen named deities arranged outward from Brahma at the centre, each said to govern a zone of the home and a matching part of the body. He admits this is only a sketch, not the full system, and the transcript here is too unclear in places to reconstruct every name reliably, so only the clearer points are kept. {{t:00:07:23}} {{t:00:08:26}} {{t:00:38:27}}

  • Brahma, the centre. Mapped to the navel and to bile; anything that turns yellow in the body (jaundice, yellow urine) is tied to this placement, along with the first "cooking" of a baby in the womb. {{t:00:08:57}} {{t:00:09:29}}
  • The south-west, the ancestors' zone (pitru-sthan). He says this area should stay quiet and undisturbed, with no main entrance there, since it governs the help ancestors can give. {{t:00:17:23}} {{t:00:17:53}}
  • A north-west zone he calls the "sulking room" (kop bhavan). A bedroom placed there is said to bring frustration, stress, depression and thyroid trouble, especially for girls in the house. {{t:00:18:57}}
  • He is explicit that remembering every deity's name is not the point; knowing a zone's general character well enough to act on it is what actually helps day to day. {{t:00:07:56}} {{t:00:19:31}}
  • He warns that upsetting the wrong deity in this grid is tied, in his account, to a long list of possible physical complaints: skin disease, eye trouble, stomach upset, headaches and nerve or leg pain among them. {{t:00:12:29}} {{t:00:13:01}}
  • Towards the north-west he also places what he calls an asura (demon) zone, home to the fiercer, "terminator" type energies in his scheme, which he keeps separate from the gentler deities nearer the doorway. {{t:00:17:53}} {{t:00:18:26}}

Several deity names and the English mnemonic phrases he uses to recall them are garbled in the source captions and are not repeated here rather than guessed at.
