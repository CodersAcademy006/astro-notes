1. Why one person gets everything: others are praying for you {{t:00:00:33}}

This class continues the Rahu and Ketu series and is about Ketu. The speaker opens with a story. A man has no faith, never visits a temple, and is wildly successful and busy helping people. The devotees complain to Vishnu. The answer: hundreds of people are asking on his behalf, while only one is asking for himself. {{t:00:00:33}}

The channel opens with a standard notice that it does not promote drugs, alcohol or intoxicants, and that the content is for information and education only. {{t:00:00:00}}

Problem

We worship and beg and get little, while someone who does not pray gets abundance. {{t:00:01:03}}

Cause and logic

In the story, Vishnu says it is the work of Rahu and Ketu, agents of Saturn, who is the son of the Sun. The Sun creates the world and Saturn judges. People think chanting a god's name will bring good, but the justice runs through Saturn. {{t:00:01:33}}

Remedy

Pray and work for those who are asking for you. Serve the world and the world asks on your behalf. If you want unlimited comfort and money, serve people. {{t:00:03:07}}

  • Rahu and Ketu as labour. Rahu is skilled labour: the head that can see. Ketu is unskilled labour: the body that breaks stones. The owner (Saturn) plans the project, such as laying railway tracks, and gives the order. {{t:00:02:04}}
  • One person asks Vishnu for himself, while hundreds ask for the other: "may his life be long, may his storehouses stay full". {{t:00:02:35}}
  • Ketu has hands and feet but no head. It feels its way by groping. {{t:00:03:07}}
  • In the face Ketu holds the tongue, the ears and the upper part of the nose. At the last moment of life the tongue still works. Old people ask for golgappa water or ghevar, and they want you to sit and listen because the tongue keeps going. An ICU patient still hands out advice. {{t:00:03:37}}
  • A small baby babbles. Hearing and speech come through Ketu, and the baby's Rahu is trained that way to speak, understand and think. {{t:00:04:08}}
  • If hundreds of hands work for you, you need not beg God. Do not ask alone: build an army of people who ask for you. {{t:00:04:39}}

The story and its moral are his teaching. It does not say that prayer by others guarantees any outcome, or that success proves goodness.
