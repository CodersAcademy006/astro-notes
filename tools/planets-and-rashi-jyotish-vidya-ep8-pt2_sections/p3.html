6. Ketu: the deity of incompleteness, and a real productivity trick {{t:01:40:34}}

Ketu is called the deity of everything unfinished: a miscarriage, an abandoned house, a job done at 75 percent, a partnership where one side is a silent partner. Anything incomplete in life is read as Ketu's signature. {{t:01:42:37}}

The problem

Expecting 100 percent completion from yourself in anything leads to constant frustration, because the speaker says 100 percent effort essentially never happens in real life. {{t:01:43:08}}

Why, in his framework

Ketu has no head: it cannot plan or decide, only act on what it is told, so any task left to Ketu alone comes out partial. {{t:01:41:05}}

The fix he actually gives

Plan for only 60 to 75 percent of a task yourself, delegate or partner out the rest, and let others finish it. His claim: doing this can bring in one and a half times the income with more free time, because it works with Ketu's nature instead of against it. {{t:01:43:08}}

8. A few questions from the class {{t:02:11:51}}

Should trust go to Krishna or to astrology? {{t:02:11:51}}

The speaker says the real question is learning what trust even means: a wrong astrologer gives wrong readings regardless of which one you "trust", so the discernment matters more than the label. {{t:02:11:51}}

Gift versus donation versus grace: are they the same? {{t:02:16:36}}

No. A donation (daan) is something given by a giver, a gift is an exchange between people, and grace (kripa) can come from anyone, even a small child's hug, and is a separate thing from both. {{t:02:16:36}}

Why does a planet-related item given as a gift feel different from one given as a formal donation? {{t:02:16:36}}

Because the speaker ties each to a different deity and context; the mechanism is the same multiplying logic described for donations in Part 1, not a new rule. {{t:02:16:36}}
