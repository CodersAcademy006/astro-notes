2. Jupiter: the household's gravity, and its one danger sign {{t:00:01:38}}

Jupiter is pictured as an old, white-haired, learned Brahmin: the planet of gravity, magnetism, knowledge and the North East of a home. The speaker ties the word "guru" to gurutva, gravitational pull. {{t:00:01:38}}

  • The Jupiter finger and the liver. In palmistry the Jupiter finger is the one used to point while asserting a demand ("listen to me"), and the speaker claims a direct link from this finger to the liver, which he calls the seat of the ego. A fatty liver is linked to ego and anger rather than diet alone, in his telling: "drink or do not, but do not carry ego, and your liver will not fail." {{t:00:04:50}}
  • Combust Jupiter (Jupiter too close to the Sun). When Jupiter's own light is washed out by the Sun's glare, a person becomes highly argumentative and impossible to convince, losing their temper over small things. {{t:00:04:18}}

The claim that distant planets transmit a direct, Wi-Fi-like vibration that affects the body (his own comparison) is the speaker's own belief, not an established scientific mechanism.

3. Jupiter and Venus as husband and wife, and Venus sign by sign {{t:00:17:14}}

The metaphor

The speaker casts Jupiter as husband (knowledge, discipline) and Venus as wife (pleasure, indulgence): "whoever can argue back is the real spouse", since a partner who never pushes back is read as a fling, not a marriage. {{t:00:17:45}}

Why they clash

Venus creates doubt, Jupiter creates stubbornness, and the two are natural opposites (he also drops an aside linking this to religious colour symbolism, which is his own opinion, not astrology). {{t:00:18:16}}

The practical fix for a quarrel

Either answer firmly once, or simply satisfy the other person's ego ("you're right") and move on; a spouse who always insists on having the last word keeps their own Jupiter disturbed. {{t:00:20:25}}
