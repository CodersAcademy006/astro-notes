11. Reading the Hanuman Chalisa: practice, diet and gender claims {{t:01:47:39}}

  • His recommended practice. Light a sesame or jasmine-oil lamp, apply sindoor with real feeling, sit facing north or east on a kusha-grass mat (which he calls an energy insulator from the earth), and recite in order: an invocation of the guru, then Hanuman, with full attention rather than mechanical repetition. {{t:01:49:47}}
  • He says the Chalisa can be read any number of times from one to many, "with devotion," and pushes back on a rule he attributes to a contemporary guru that it must be read exactly three times, saying repetition count matters less than understanding what is actually being recited. {{t:01:53:30}}
  • Diet while worshipping Hanuman. He claims alcohol, meat and casual sex are "forbidden" for someone actively worshipping Hanuman, not because the items are evil in themselves, but because engaging them keeps the lower chakras active, which he says conflicts with building Hanuman energy at the navel. {{t:02:00:27}}
  • He is careful to separate worshipping Hanuman from embodying "Hanuman consciousness": reciting the Chalisa without living a disciplined, protective character is, in his words, empty repetition rather than real devotion. {{t:02:13:29}}

The strongest claims in this part of the talk concern women specifically: he says women should generally not actively worship Hanuman because it is said to activate "male energy" and disrupt reproductive health, that a woman's navel should never fully touch the ground in prostration (unlike a man's), and that menstruating women should not enter a Hindu temple (though he notes Sikh gurdwaras make an exception). He separately claims women who feel no pride in being a woman are prone to ovarian or uterine disease, and men obsessed only with masculinity are prone to prostate disease. These are the speaker's own traditional and gendered claims. These notes record them because they were said, do not endorse any of them, and give no medical advice of any kind. Readers with any health concern should see a qualified doctor regardless of gender. {{t:01:47:08}}
