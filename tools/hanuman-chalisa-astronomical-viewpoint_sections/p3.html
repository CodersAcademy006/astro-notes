5. DNA, vanars and "modified beings" {{t:00:25:49}}

This entire section is the speaker's own speculative reading of mythology through pop-science DNA percentages. The specific DNA-similarity figures he cites (shown below) are commonly repeated claims that do not match the scientific consensus, and the conclusions he draws from them (ancient genetic engineering, "modified" vanars) are his own belief, not science. These notes record what he said without verifying or endorsing any of it. {{t:00:25:49}}

His starting claim: human DNA is around 60 percent similar to a banana, 80 percent to a cow, about 95 percent to a dog, rat or elephant, and roughly 98 to 99 percent to a chimpanzee. He treats humanity's edge over its nearest animal relative as roughly "one percent," and argues that this one percent already explains the entire gap between an ape and a civilisation that builds satellites and rockets. {{t:00:26:20}}

  • From there, he reasons that any being with even a few percent more "DNA" than a human would look like a god to us, the way we already look god-like to a chimpanzee. {{t:00:27:55}}
  • He applies this to the vanaras (monkey-formed beings) of the Ramayana: figures like Nal, Neel and Angad are introduced as sons of named deities (Vishwakarma, the Ashwini twins), which he reads as evidence of deliberate "cross-mutation" between humans and animals in that era. {{t:00:29:28}}
  • He cites a claim, attributed to a YouTube creator he does not name in detail, about South Indian temple carvings mixing human and animal forms (cow-human, dog-human, lion-fish), reading these as records of the same ancient cross-breeding. {{t:00:30:32}}
  • Hanuman himself is described in this framework as the most refined result of this process, son of Pawan (wind) and, in the mythology he cites, linked to Indra's lineage, said to be why Hanuman's own DNA is "miraculous." {{t:00:31:37}}
