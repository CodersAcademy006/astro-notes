3. The Sun: confidence, vitamin D, and the only planets that can touch it {{t:00:20:13}}

The speaker maps the right side of the body to the Sun's channel and the left to the Moon's, then spends real time on why the Sun is, in his account, the one planet almost nothing can defeat head-on. {{t:00:20:13}} {{t:00:22:51}}

When the Sun is weak

Low confidence, low vitamin D, tired eyes, body aches, a constant feeling of helplessness: "I can't do anything about him, about her." {{t:00:26:30}} {{t:00:27:00}}

His reasoning

The Sun is the father, the self, the navel's fire, and the eyes. Sunlight eaten through food becomes the body's own "Sun shakti" at the cellular level, making red and white blood cells. Only Rahu and Ketu, the shadow planets, can ever eclipse it. {{t:00:21:17}} {{t:00:21:48}} {{t:00:22:51}}

Remedy

Stop blaming other people and surrender the outcome, which he frames as devotion to Krishna or whichever deity you choose, so the Sun is not dragged down by resentment. {{t:00:27:31}} {{t:00:28:02}}

  • He calls Leo (Simha rashi) the one sign that cannot be dominated by any other sign, since it is the Sun's own house. {{t:00:22:20}}
  • A solar eclipse happens because Rahu, the severed head, swallows the Sun; the same logic runs inside a person: when Rahu disturbs your chart, you feel you can see, speak and hear, yet cannot act. {{t:00:22:51}} {{t:00:26:30}}
  • Low vitamin D shows up as tiredness, dental and bone trouble and eye strain, which he reads as the Sun depleting because of that constant feeling of helplessness. {{t:00:27:00}} {{t:00:28:02}}
  • If your actual father is difficult, drunk, abusive or absent, his suggestion is to mentally "adopt" another father figure: a grandfather's photo, a chosen elder, or God as the parent, so the Sun's placement is not left starving. {{t:00:28:34}}
  • In the home's vastu, he places the father's and ancestors' zone where Rahu sits, so keeping that area calm and respected is, in his account, part of keeping the Sun steady too. {{t:00:28:34}} {{t:00:29:06}}

The advice to mentally replace a difficult parent, and the framing of helplessness as a spiritual rather than medical condition, are the speaker's own views. Low vitamin D or persistent fatigue are medical matters for a doctor, not an astrology remedy.

4. The Moon: your mother, your immunity, and what not to give away {{t:00:29:39}}

After a short detour through Rahu as Saturn's agent and Ketu as Hanuman's tail, the class settles into its longest single-planet block: the Moon as mother, water and the body's white blood cells. {{t:00:29:39}} {{t:00:37:33}}

Two quick asides before the Moon {{t:00:29:39}}

  • Rahu as Saturn's agent. He pictures Rahu as a whisky bottle: respect it, do not necessarily drink it, and never kick it over. Saturn and the Sun are cast as father and son in conflict, with Rahu as the "illegitimate" agent who settles Saturn's grudge against the Sun. {{t:00:29:39}} {{t:00:30:11}}
  • Ketu as Hanuman's tail. The story of Hanuman burning Lanka with his tail, not his hands, stands in for Ketu doing the work while Mars (Hanuman) directs it; if Mars is set well, Ketu performs well too. He adds that vehicles represent this service energy, so keeping your car or bike clean is said to keep Saturn's staff, Rahu and Ketu, content. {{t:00:30:11}} {{t:00:31:45}} {{t:00:32:47}}

When the Moon is afflicted

Skin eruptions and marks, a restless, indecisive mind that flips its opinion of the same person every couple of hours, and in his most extreme claim, cancer risk. {{t:00:41:13}} {{t:00:43:52}} {{t:00:45:29}}

His reasoning

The Moon is water, the mother, and governs the body's full white-blood-cell and lymph system, the immune system's raw material. A troubled relationship with your mother, he says, is read the same way as a troubled Moon. {{t:00:37:33}} {{t:00:39:08}} {{t:00:40:41}}

Remedy

If your mother could not love you well for whatever reason, he suggests mentally "adopting" another mother figure, the same cord-cutting idea used for a difficult father. Keep white, watery things (milk, water, white cloth) away from careless use when the Moon is weak. {{t:00:40:09}} {{t:00:40:41}}

  • Do not donate your strong Moon's things. If the Moon is exalted or in a friendly sign, giving away water, milk or white cloth is said to bring loss rather than merit; giving away a weak planet's item is the one that helps. Fear itself is read here too: a frightened mind is said to shrink the kidneys and bring urinary trouble. {{t:00:49:07}} {{t:00:49:40}}
  • Old grudges, old clothes. He compares nursing a year-old insult to wearing a year-old unwashed shirt: both are things we would not actually want, yet the mind holds on anyway. Letting the Moon carry fewer old thoughts is itself a remedy. {{t:00:49:53}} {{t:00:50:12}}
  • Lactose intolerance is, in his account, tied to a disturbed Moon, and fire-element people who love very spicy food may react badly to dairy for the same reason. This is his own framework, not a medical diagnosis. {{t:00:52:46}} {{t:00:53:18}}
  • Toilets in the north to north-east belt of a home are, in his telling, the single biggest Moon-dosha trigger, capable in the worst cases of a cancer dosha, and more commonly skin eruptions and a troubled mind. {{t:00:45:29}} {{t:00:46:00}}
  • Girlfriend versus wife. He assigns the 5th house to a girlfriend or boyfriend and the 7th house to a spouse, so a strong 5th house and a strong 7th house can point to two different outcomes for the same person. {{t:00:48:35}}
  • The remedy stones here are a pearl (moti) for the Moon, worn on the smallest finger, and a ruby (manikya) for the Sun, mentioned only in passing. {{t:00:51:45}}

The claims about lactose intolerance, cancer risk from a toilet's placement, and treating an estranged parent as replaceable are the speaker's personal and traditional beliefs. None of this is medical advice, and a toilet's location does not cause cancer.
