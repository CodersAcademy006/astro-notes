11. Questions: guru, surrender and stones as damage control {{t:01:24:42}}

Can witness mind itself be a kind of hypnosis? {{t:01:23:40}}

Yes, he says: witness mind (sakshi bhav) is itself a form of conscious "hypnosis" you choose, where you notice your own anger or confusion as it happens. His example: admitting "I don't know this, please tell me" instead of staying embarrassed is sakshi bhav in action, since shame has dropped away. {{t:01:24:10}}

Do I need a guru, and does Jiddu Krishnamurti's "follow no one" advice contradict that? {{t:01:29:53}}

His answer: there is no single rule for everyone. Some people need a guru who works through a kind of hypnosis/devotion, some don't, and different teachers suit different people. He says a teacher who tells people to reject all gurus is also, in effect, a guru for the people that approach suits. If a person has real substance, you won't be able to stop yourself from bowing to them; a forced, token guru-disciple relationship changes nothing. {{t:01:30:24}}

Who is doing the seeking, if "munna" (the mind) is also doing the watching? {{t:01:31:59}}

His answer collapses the question: the part that wants witness mind, wants moksha, and watches the mind is still the mind (munna) watching itself. He compares it to a well-worn marriage or friendship: a comfortable "give and take" settles in, not a once-and-for-all transcendence. {{t:01:32:30}}

Which hand should a gemstone go on? {{t:01:35:35}}

His rule of thumb: right hand for men, left for women, tied to which brain hemisphere he associates with each sex. He is explicit that this is only a general rule and can be switched by trial: "if it isn't settling, try the other hand." {{t:01:36:05}}

Associating a dominant brain hemisphere with sex is the speaker's own claim, not an established finding; these notes do not endorse it.

If a stone "didn't work" for someone, or a non-gem-wearer still succeeded, what does that mean? {{t:01:36:36}}

Claim

People blame a stone for a later loss, or point to famous, successful people who are rarely seen wearing gemstones, as proof stones don't matter. {{t:01:37:37}}

His logic

A stone, in his claim, only ever limits downside ("damage control"); it was never going to create success that wasn't otherwise due. Someone whose own discipline and fate already carry them doesn't need the limiter. Helmets, glasses and donations work the same way in his analogy: no visible gain, only reduced loss when something goes wrong. {{t:01:37:07}}

His takeaway

Consciousness (strict routine, discipline, or simply a settled fate) can substitute for a stone; the stone is one of several equally valid "easy ways out", not a unique fix. {{t:01:38:39}}

He extends the same logic to unconventional health choices: people who avoid hospitals and rely instead on herbal remedies and stay well, in his view, are simply living out their own fixed fate (prarabdh), not proof that the remedy itself was responsible. {{t:01:38:08}}

Comparisons to specific wealthy or powerful public figures as evidence either way are the speaker's own examples; the figures are not named in these notes. These are his opinions about money, success and alternative health choices, not financial or medical advice, and these notes do not endorse avoiding medical care.

Should I keep astrology consultations private? {{t:01:40:13}}

His general advice, as the moderator restates it: avoid broadcasting the specifics of a personal reading or remedy to others. (The exact context of this rule was unclear in the recording; treat it as a general preference for privacy around remedies rather than a strict law.) {{t:01:40:46}}

Do departed ancestors (pitr) ever cause harm, the way living parents can? {{t:01:41:18}}

His claim: a living parent, with their own unmet needs, can be a source of friction; a deceased parent, in his framework, exists only at a chromosomal/DNA level and "wants" nothing, so the two relationships are not comparable. He says that if the south-west zone of a home (associated with ancestors in his system) is neglected, the household can suffer for it. {{t:01:41:49}}

Claims about departed ancestors acting through DNA are the speaker's personal belief, not a scientific claim.

A 9th-5th house swap (navapancham yoga) {{t:01:42:20}}

Asked to explain a chart combination where the 9th house's ruler sits in the 5th house and the 5th house's ruler sits in the 9th, he says the result depends entirely on which actual planets are involved and their own nature, not on the swap by itself; he offers to work through a real chart example later rather than give one general rule. {{t:01:42:52}}

How does a devotee become a "sakha" (friend) of a deity, rather than a servant? {{t:01:43:22}}

His answer, offered half playfully: closeness of that kind comes only once ego stops keeping score of status; he jokes that calling a deity "friend" can feel like a demotion from imagining yourself equal to them. More seriously, he says finding peace in someone's absence, not only their presence, is what the "friend" bond (sakha bhava) means to him. {{t:01:44:26}}

This exchange is affectionate religious banter between the speaker and a questioner about Hindu deities; it is presented here as his personal reflection, not religious instruction.

Gemstones versus faith in your own consciousness: an "either/or"? {{t:01:45:58}}

A listener who had removed gemstones and rings after an earlier class asked whether consciousness alone is "more effective" than a stone. His answer: trusting your own judgement over all advice, including his own, is simply ego dressed up as faith, not real wisdom; he predicts the person will eventually return to the stones. {{t:01:46:30}}

Can a disturbed Moon still wear silver? {{t:01:47:00}}

No, in his rule: if the Moon is afflicted anywhere in the chart, avoid wearing silver on the body; he allows other remedies such as worshipping Shiva or bathing with Gangajal (Ganges water) brought home, which he says fully addresses Rahu-related doshas in his claim. {{t:01:47:32}}

Is marriage the "safest, easiest" path in life? {{t:01:48:03}}

He calls marriage a socially easy default, not a guarantee of happiness; a person can live fulfilled without it, but marriage settles "the society question" in one step. {{t:01:48:03}}

This is the speaker's personal opinion about marriage and society, not relationship advice.

Does an IVF process put a toll on the body, not just money? {{t:01:48:34}}

Asked whether IVF costs the body as well as money, he says yes: when a chart dosha (he names Saturn, Pluto or Ketu as candidates) blocks natural conception, the same dosha can make an IVF cycle fail, and in his claim even "take a toll" physically, sometimes repeating across a few attempts before succeeding. He also claims certain severe chart combinations are linked to conditions he names as Down syndrome (via Pluto) or autism (via Neptune) in his framework.

These are the speaker's personal astrological beliefs about fertility treatment and about the causes of Down syndrome and autism. They are not medical facts, are not endorsed by these notes, and should never replace advice from a qualified fertility specialist or doctor. No dosing or treatment guidance is given here, nor should any be taken from the original video.

What about performing your own last rites (shraddh) while alive, to end suffering? {{t:01:51:06}}

Asked whether a person stuck with an "unsolvable" chart dosha could perform their own shraddh (a ritual normally done after death) to find peace, he says it would do nothing: the chart's influence continues regardless of ritual, and he discourages the idea as empty theatre, redirecting the listener toward finding a capable guide instead. {{t:01:51:37}}

This question touches on ending suffering through ritual; the speaker's answer does not address mental health directly. Readers in distress should contact a qualified professional or a crisis helpline rather than rely on ritual or astrology.

Who is a saint (sant), and how do you recognize one? {{t:01:55:46}}

His answer: "sant" comes from "sat" (truth), and he applies it broadly across traditions, to Guru Nanak, to the Buddha, to Kabir, to anyone who has known the truth and lives by it, regardless of their formal religion. {{t:01:55:46}}

He defines knowing the truth as accepting that the universe runs on its own, "auto-pilot", with someone or something else behind the controls; a true sant has no complaints and divides nothing into right or wrong, since that duality is, in his claim, simply how creation works. {{t:01:56:17}}

He says a sant looks and behaves ordinary, not performed or theatrical, and follows what he calls "sahaj yog" (an easy, natural path) rather than "hath yog" (forced effort or willpower); he includes Guru Nanak's marriage and children, and Kabir's own domestic life, as proof that sainthood does not require renouncing ordinary life. {{t:01:57:19}}

His summary: a sant can still enjoy life fully; the only mark of sainthood he insists on is living by the truth without blame, judgement or drama. {{t:01:58:23}}

Fate, free will and about 12 percent {{t:02:17:08}}

Asked whether strong personal faith can override a negative chart reading, he says a fixed portion, which he puts at about 12 percent of a life, plays out regardless. His examples are religious and historical figures whose fates, in his account, were not altered by status or faith, alongside a comparison to a time-travel film character who sees many possible futures but still finds "everything has a payment". {{t:02:17:08}}

The 12 percent figure and the claims about named religious and historical figures' deaths are the speaker's own belief framework, offered without a verifiable source.

12. A worked chart: how the same dosha plays out {{t:01:58:53}}

The class closes with the speaker reading a live chart as a teaching example. It is a real consultation, so these notes summarise only the method he demonstrates (how a retrograde or combust planet changes its reading, how a house's ruler sitting in another house redirects its results) and compress the many repeated gemstone and thread remedies into one line each. Specific personal predictions about one individual's marriage, fertility or finances are the speaker's own chart reading, not a general rule, and are not a professional opinion on anyone's real situation.

  • Retrograde and combust planets. A planet very close to the Sun is "combust" (ast) and its effect is suppressed; a retrograde planet's influence is delayed or reversed rather than expressed directly. His rule: both combust and retrograde placements respond better to a gemstone than a placement that is neither. {{t:02:18:12}}
  • A house ruler's seat decides the house's story. He repeatedly traces a house's outcome (marriage, career, father's relationship) by locating that house's ruling planet elsewhere in the chart and reading its strength and company there, rather than only looking at the house itself. {{t:02:02:34}}
  • He reads a Jupiter sitting alongside another planet as never allowing either friendship or conflict to fully resolve, since Jupiter (in his framework) blocks both outcomes equally. {{t:01:59:57}}
  • Marital strain, in-law interference and difficulty divorcing are, in his reading of this chart, tied to Saturn's placement over the relationship house, which he says can block a clean separation even when the marriage is unhappy. {{t:02:04:41}}
  • He repeats that this isn't the individual's own choice either way: in his reading, Saturn decides both whether the marriage ends and on what terms, not the people in it. {{t:02:05:11}}
  • For remedies he repeats through the reading: a sapphire (nilam) for Pluto/Saturn-type trouble, an emerald (panna) for a weak Mercury, red thread on the right foot and black thread on the left for specific house support, almonds offered at a place of worship, and burying soaked gulkand (rose preserve) in open, shadeless ground for several weeks. {{t:02:07:48}}
  • A ruler thrown from its own house. When a house's ruling planet sits in the house just before it (the 12th from itself), he reads that house's resources as being swept away by the very planet meant to protect them. {{t:02:02:03}}
  • A squeezed house. Where a house sits sandwiched between two difficult outer planets on either side (his example: Neptune and Pluto flanking the relationship house), he reads the house as structurally compromised however it is treated directly. {{t:02:02:34}}
  • A placement with no supportive aspect on it at all, he says, leaves the body part it governs exposed; his example here reads an unprotected Mars aspect as a cue for shoulder or ear trouble. {{t:02:02:34}}
  • Which relative gets involved changes the outcome. In his reading of this chart, conflict escalates further when the spouse's father is drawn in, and eases somewhat if it passes through the maternal side instead. {{t:02:12:31}}
  • A house ruler sitting in a house of open enmity (the 6th), he reads as the person needing an actual rival to "activate" success in that house, rather than a quiet life being the better outcome. {{t:02:16:41}}
  • A retrograde Saturn aspecting the house of study, in his reading of this chart, all but forces a change of subject or field along the way. {{t:02:01:32}}
  • Despite several faults elsewhere in the same chart, he says a very strong, fully-aspecting Mars keeps money flowing for whatever is spent, regardless of the other weaknesses. {{t:02:02:03}}
  • Where the relationship house and the house just after it both come under affliction, he reads the person as both creating enemies and eventually overcoming them, rather than one or the other. {{t:02:03:06}}
  • Checking the navamsa separately. Even where the main chart's relationship-house ruler looks acceptable, he checks its placement again in the navamsa (a secondary divisional chart); a hostile navamsa placement there is what he blames for constant bedroom conflict in this case. {{t:02:08:52}}
  • A badly placed Saturn, he claims, can "blind" a person to their own spending entirely, so they cannot account for where or why money left their hands. {{t:02:15:40}}
  • He adds that Pluto, as the figure governing death in his scheme, does not distinguish between religions or faiths in who it takes. {{t:02:14:36}}
  • His closing analogy for a gemstone: like resting through a fever rather than curing it outright, a stone does not remove a dosha, it only eases the symptom while the underlying cycle runs its course. {{t:02:19:13}}

The suggestion in this reading that an unhappy marriage could be eased by keeping an additional partner is the speaker's personal opinion stated to one individual in a live reading. These notes do not endorse this or any similar relationship advice, and nothing here is legal advice on marriage or divorce.
