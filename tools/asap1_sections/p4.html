9. Ketu: the dog, the fakir and the lower body {{t:01:03:47}}

The pattern

A dog won't eat from your hand, or barks at and troubles you; skin disease; back, tailbone or lower-body pain; a bitten tongue; broken bins or footwear at home. {{t:01:06:57}}

Why it is Ketu

Ketu is the headless body: it can act but not judge, pictured as a dog on a leash following wherever the "head" (a partner, friend or leader) pulls. It governs everything below the neck, the tongue, skin, and the capacity to enjoy anything bodily. {{t:01:06:26}}

Consciousness shift

Feed a dog; it is, in his claim, the simplest way to please Ketu. He widens this to feeding cows, crows, wandering fakirs and saints, and setting aside a guest's share before eating, though he does not detail that remedy fully in this talk. {{t:01:07:58}}

  • Who counts as a "fakir" or saint. Not someone in a particular costume, but someone who has stopped begging and says "that's enough" when given something; he calls this "pure Ketu". Robes and ritual appearance, in his view, are theatre and not the substance of sainthood. {{t:01:08:30}}
  • A small accident that leaves you unhurt, he reads as Ketu's grace; a severe accident with only a scratch is the same grace at a larger scale. A bitten tongue signals the exact moment Ketu is disturbed, worth a quick bow to the dog or to Bhairav (Ketu's deity, linked to the south-west). {{t:01:07:27}}
  • His mythological anchor for the dog image: the same loyal, headless dog later walks with Yudhishthira all the way to heaven, which he reads as Ketu faithfully following a righteous head to the very end. {{t:01:07:21}}
  • Eating in bed is fine in itself; what unsettles Ketu, in his claim, is when a bed used for intimacy is also used as a dining table, since the two functions interfere with each other by his account. {{t:01:09:33}}
  • If a married child still will not treat you as a parent, or a daughter never fully "becomes" a daughter in her manner, he reads that as a Mercury or Jupiter fault rather than Ketu, since Ketu's own domain stops at the lower body and its enjoyment. {{t:01:10:04}}
  • Discarding a broken dustbin is, in his claim, fine and even useful: a bin itself is linked to Ketu, so a damaged one is best thrown out rather than kept in use. {{t:01:09:33}}
  • A skin complaint, a mouth ulcer or a heated, irritable stomach are all, in his scheme, Ketu disturbances; he separates this from the sexual and digestive functions Ketu also governs. {{t:01:09:33}}
  • He widens "guru" here to mean whoever first triggers a real shift in a person's awareness, whether that is a formal teacher, a sibling, a parent, or even a daughter-in-law. {{t:01:05:22}}
  • Specific dietary customs tied to a day of the week, he says, apply only to someone whose own Venus is already compromised; he claims a person with a clean Venus placement can eat something others are warned against without the same consequence. {{t:01:11:06}}

Associating meat-eating customs or specific diets with planetary doshas is the speaker's personal belief framework. These notes do not endorse or discourage any dietary or religious practice on that basis.

10. Indra, Neptune and Pluto: enjoyment, grace and death {{t:01:12:39}}

Past the nine classical grahas, the speaker folds in three more figures from his own framework: Indra for enjoyment, Neptune for grace, and Pluto for death and chronic illness.

Indra: enjoyment blocked

A dirty, cluttered house; money sitting in the account while the person still travels by the cheapest option out of habit; never actually using what you've earned. He claims this is also a leading cause he associates with cancer, through chronic clutter in the home's south, south-west and north-east zones. {{t:01:12:39}}

Neptune: grace missing

Neither medicine nor alcohol seems to "work" on the person, in the folk phrase he quotes: "medicine won't touch him, try liquor instead." That double failure, in his claim, signals someone with no grace (kripa) left to draw on. {{t:01:17:52}}

Pluto: illness and fear

A string of small, recurring health troubles that never clear up fully is, in his claim, Pluto unsettled; he calls Pluto the "lord of death" and ties it to the chronic, slow kind of suffering rather than a single acute illness. {{t:01:21:33}}

  • Indra's fix: use what you have. He says the remedy is simply to spend on comfort you can afford instead of hoarding out of fear, and to keep the house clean, especially wardrobes and cupboards nobody opens. {{t:01:14:14}}
  • Neptune's fix: "party mode". His claim is that an attitude of generosity and enjoyment, treating friends well, not counting small costs, invites grace; stinginess over trivial amounts is, in his view, the sign of a poor Neptune. {{t:01:18:55}}
  • Pluto's fix: simple devotion. He suggests bowing toward the south, or lighting a lamp there, as a minimal practice, grouping Pluto with a mythological role as the god of justice/dharma (Dharmaraj). {{t:01:23:08}}
  • He adds that chanting, kirtan or devotional singing is, in his claim, a way of keeping all three of these forces (Indra, Neptune, Pluto) in balance at once. {{t:01:23:08}}
  • He contrasts how much ease a small child enjoys, delighting in simple play, with how little ease an adult allows themselves, and calls this difference in attitude the real gap in "party mode", not a difference in actual circumstances. {{t:01:19:25}}
  • Extreme stinginess over very small sums, in his telling, is itself the clearest sign of a weak Neptune, regardless of a person's actual wealth. {{t:01:19:57}}
  • Being overly selective or stingy about who or what you will accept, he warns, blocks Neptune; staying open-handed, even if it looks careless to others, is in his claim what keeps work and opportunity moving. {{t:01:20:28}}
  • He claims people born before 1960 systematically carry a weaker Indra than people today, reasoning that extreme personal wealth used to be far rarer in that generation. {{t:01:14:45}}
  • He treats the three figures as converted forms of the same underlying force: the giver of grace (Varuna/Neptune), the giver of pleasure (Indra), and the one who ultimately "settles the account" through death or dharma (Pluto/Dharmaraj). {{t:01:22:37}}

Linking household clutter to a cancer diagnosis is the speaker's personal belief and is not a medical claim. Anyone concerned about cancer risk or a diagnosis should consult a qualified doctor; nothing here should delay medical care. The claim that people born before 1960 generally have a weaker Indra is the speaker's own generational generalisation about wealth, not a verified economic or astrological finding.
