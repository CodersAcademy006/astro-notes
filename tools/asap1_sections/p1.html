1. Knowledge, wisdom and the first lesson on consciousness {{t:00:00:41}}

This is the opening class of a new series the speaker calls ASAP, built around consciousness (chetana) rather than remedies alone. His starting claim: a person's awareness of their own life can move a chart's results, and the class will spend the rest of the talk showing this planet by planet.

Knowledge (नॉलेज)

Comes from books and outside sources. It has a ceiling: you can finish learning a subject. {{t:00:00:41}}

Wisdom (ज्ञान)

Rises from inside a person and has no limit. The speaker says every year of his own life he still feels he knows nothing yet. {{t:00:00:41}}

The guru's job

A guru is only a guide to the next stretch of road, not a stopping point. Stopping at a guru, a temple, an idol, a daughter-in-law or a spouse keeps the same grief (klesh) running. {{t:00:01:12}}

  • The speaker's own framing: do your karma and let "the owner" (Vishnu) resolve the rest, since Vishnu does all the acting and Lakshmi stays at his feet. Money is important: those who say money is nothing usually have plenty of it, and those who worship poverty usually have too much lying idle. {{t:00:01:43}}
  • He suggests working for money honestly until the urge to give some away arises on its own, rather than assuming you already have enough. {{t:00:02:46}}
  • His picture of the soul's journey: we get stuck at a guru, a temple or a person for lifetimes, and each time we start to let go, "God or munna" hands us a fresh grief, story or tangle to stay stuck on. {{t:00:02:14}}
  • Early success is a trap. His example: a business or relationship that succeeds too easily early on sets a benchmark that nothing later can beat, so later, larger success stops giving any real pleasure. He suggests welcoming early failure instead, as protection against this trap. {{t:00:04:18}}
  • He reads being "beaten" or humbled early in life as a kind of initiation, comparing it to Arjuna's despair before receiving Krishna's teaching: knowledge, in his telling, is not handed out freely but after hardship. {{t:00:04:50}}
  • His definition of "chaitanya" (awakened consciousness). Simply knowing what you are: a child who knows he is a child is a "wise child", and a woman who knows she is a woman is a "wise woman". Believing yourself special, divine or above the rules, in his view, is not elevated consciousness but a different problem altogether. {{t:00:03:47}}

The remark that people who dismiss money's importance "have plenty of it" and vice versa is the speaker's personal generalisation, not a verified claim about money or psychology.

2. The Sun: a king does not beg {{t:00:06:22}}

The speaker opens the planet-by-planet tour with the Sun, built around one behaviour: begging versus owning your right (hak) to ask.

The pattern

Someone who is always begging, from God, from a bank clerk, from a relative, is acting like a subject, not a king. A king bows to God with gratitude, not demands. {{t:00:06:22}}

Why it is Sun

The Sun is "our God", the centre that keeps the solar system alive and the giver of unlimited, continuous light (photons). A weak Sun in the chart, he says, shows up in people who spend their life begging. {{t:00:06:22}}

Consciousness shift

Recognise you have a right (hak) to ask, the way a child assumes a right over a parent. Dropping the sin/virtue duality (dvait) in your own head is, in his framing, what keeps the Sun strong. {{t:00:07:58}}

  • He places the Sun's East direction (E5 in his own shorthand) at the house's east side, tied to the honour of the home's elders. {{t:00:07:27}}
  • Everyone who begs through religious ritual all their life, in his reading, has a troubled Sun; the fault is not about one incident but a running pattern. {{t:00:07:27}}
  • Duality (dvait). He claims people who stop dividing everything into "good" and "bad" rise to the top and keep a Sun-type confidence, giving a currently prominent world leader in his eighties as his example of someone, in his view, free of that duality. {{t:00:08:30}}
  • Right eye is Sun, linked to the pituitary gland; left eye is Moon, linked to the pineal gland. Together he calls this pair "Goloka Dham". {{t:00:10:34}}
  • A person who scolds someone and then regrets it, in his framing, always has a weak Sun, and it can weaken the right eye (or the left, in a baby born breech). {{t:00:10:34}}
  • Street beggars, in his view, are all playing out a Saturn or Rahu role, never a Sun role, in their chart. {{t:00:11:06}}
  • Bhiksha versus bheekh. Asked whether monks and renunciates who ask for alms (bhiksha) are different from beggars (bheekh), he says yes: bhiksha is asked for as a right (dharma), and a true renunciate does not curse those who refuse, while a beggar does. {{t:00:13:46}}
  • He adds that acceptance (not mere adjustment) of life as it comes is what makes someone "a king" in this sense; whoever cannot accept family, work or circumstance ends up with a ruined Sun and a growing sense of helplessness. {{t:00:14:17}}
  • He distinguishes real confidence from ego by whether a person is even aware their confidence might look like arrogance and still proceeds without flinching; once that awareness is present, he claims, it no longer functions as ego at all. {{t:00:09:01}}

The speaker names a living world leader as his example of someone beyond "good and bad" thinking, and separately references other current political figures and events. These are his personal political opinions stated inside an astrology class, not astrological analysis, and are not repeated by name in these notes.

3. The Moon: water, immunity and the hypnosis of "should" {{t:00:11:38}}

The pattern

Water trouble at home, leaking taps, damp walls, a water tank or motor that keeps failing, especially around a daughter's visits. {{t:00:11:38}}

Why it is Moon

The body is about 72% water in his framing, and the Moon runs that water: sweat, tears, nasal and other fluids, and through them the mind (munna/man). A disturbed Moon brings low mood, and he links it to the many illnesses he groups under "blood-related". {{t:00:14:48}}

Consciousness shift

The Moon also carries immunity in his scheme. A settled Moon lets you shrug off a disagreement ("you be you, I'll be me") instead of reacting to every opinion like an allergy. {{t:00:15:20}}

  • The "it is said" hypnosis. Phrases like "it is said you shouldn't do this" or "elders don't attend after they pass" are, in his view, a collectively created hypnosis with no one claiming authorship. He ties this pattern to a disturbed Moon and the loss of mental clarity it brings. {{t:00:16:24}}
  • If the household dosha is present, this pattern of borrowed rules without clarity becomes part of the person's own temperament. {{t:00:16:56}}
  • He assigns the Moon's water, immunity and emotional role jointly to the body's fluids: sweat, urine, tears and nasal moisture, and says a Moon dosha shows up as 36,000 "blood-related" conditions in his count, including low mood. {{t:00:15:52}}
  • A person with a settled Moon, in his telling, simply walks away from a disagreement rather than needing the other person to change their view. {{t:00:16:24}}
