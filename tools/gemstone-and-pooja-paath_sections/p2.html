4. When the right prayer still goes wrong {{t:00:04:09}}

The speaker's core warning for this class: worshipping a real deity does not guarantee a good result, because you can call its negative form, or call a form that is simply not yours. {{t:00:04:09}}

His body analogies

Too much salt raises blood pressure. Too many almonds can make bones brittle. Ghee is needed by the body but can also feed a heart attack. His point: something genuinely good for people in general can still hurt you specifically, and worship works the same way. {{t:00:04:09}}

His claim about why

If you worship one deity and keep losing, the deity is still listening, but you may be reaching its negative aspect. Worshipping Ram, Krishna or Shiva can each cause harm, in his account, if it is the wrong one for your own chart. {{t:00:05:14}}

His suggested fix

Worship the formless (nirakar) instead of guessing at a form, and find a guide who actually knows this depth of the science rather than copying whatever ritual is popular. {{t:00:05:45}}

In passing, he also argues that romantic love is ultimately self-interested, that people "love" someone because they want something back, and that most affection is really a desire dressed up as devotion. This is his own personal philosophy about love and relationships, offered here only as background to the worship discussion. These notes record it because it was said, and do not endorse it as a rule for anyone's relationships. {{t:00:14:36}}

All of the outcomes in this section (brittle bones, blood pressure, heart attack, or a ruined life from the "wrong" deity) are the speaker's own analogies and traditional claims. These notes record them as his claims, not as medical fact, and give no medical advice of any kind. {{t:00:04:42}}

The office hierarchy analogy. If you walk into a government office and hand your application straight to the top director, he may simply tear it up for wasting his time; the clerk is who actually processes it, and a small token of thanks to the clerk gets the job done. The same director happily receives a friend who visits without any application at all. His point: calling on the "biggest" form of God with the wrong approach can get your request ignored the same way, while the right, humble approach to the right level gets it handled. {{t:00:12:02}}

His comparison: before food-allergy testing existed, people simply fell ill without knowing why; now a doctor runs a test and tells you to drop peanuts or gluten and the problem resolves itself, no medicine needed. A guru or a good astrologer is meant to play the same role for worship: telling you plainly what to do and what to avoid. {{t:00:07:19}}
