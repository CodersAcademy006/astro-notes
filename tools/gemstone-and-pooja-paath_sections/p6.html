11. Stone quality, cost and timing {{t:01:47:03}}

  • Clarity beats carat. A small, five-ratti stone with no visible inclusions is said to outwork a larger ten-ratti stone that is full of cracks and spots, because a flawless stone is the one actually able to filter the rays cleanly. {{t:01:47:33}}
  • His analogies for a flawed stone: drinking tea through a torn strainer, where tea leaves keep getting through, or wearing scratched glasses for years until a doctor eventually diagnoses glaucoma from the eye compensating for the damage. {{t:01:48:36}}
  • Retrograde (vakri) planets complicate the choice. A planet that is retrograde at birth but moving forward now, or the reverse, changes whether its stone helps or harms; the speaker says this detail is too complex to cover fully in this class and defers it. {{t:01:51:12}}
  • His cost philosophy: if you cannot afford a strong, high-quality stone right away, save for it rather than buying a cheap, flawed one; "cheap is never the best." A good stone, in his claim, needs no recharging and lasts for life, even centuries. {{t:01:47:33}}

Claims that a correctly worn stone "works 100%," brings guaranteed exam success or wealth, or that a disappointing result always means the stone or the house needs a stronger remedy, are the speaker's own assurances. These notes do not endorse them as guarantees and give no financial advice. {{t:02:08:00}}

He also argues that people who happily pay full price for a phone, a car or a wedding then try to haggle down a ritual's cost, and calls this the real reason remedies seem not to work: being stingy toward yourself, in his view, blocks the result as much as the wrong stone would. {{t:02:03:49}}

His closing comparison in this section, that modern surgery and medicine are why people now live to 80 instead of dying at 40, and that this is also why people no longer go to temples for smallpox or polio, is his own framing of religion versus medicine. These notes do not treat it as medical guidance, and nothing in this talk should be read as a reason to delay seeing a doctor. {{t:01:53:49}}
