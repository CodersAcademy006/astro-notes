1. Reading the signs when life will not move {{t:00:00:00}}

We plan our whole day: the interview, the deal, the house, the marriage, and then one small hour tells us somebody else is running this life. The speaker's opening point: most of our suffering comes from refusing to read the early signal that something is not going to happen yet. {{t:00:00:00}}

A surgeon will not operate on a patient who is thrashing and screaming. He first sedates the patient, then operates. God works the same way: an interview fails once, the person panics and goes in for the second one twice as agitated, fails again, gets more frantic for the third and fourth, and by then is "shouting too much" for anything to be done for them. {{t:00:01:01}}

  • His opening example of over-insistence: deciding "today I will get the job," "today I will book the marriage match," "today I will buy the house" as if willpower alone decides it, when something else may simply not be ready to send it yet. {{t:00:00:30}}
  • The usual spiral: a plan fails, we get upset, we go to the temple and bang our head on the floor, we curse the deity, then we go chasing some "guru ghantal" (a fraudulent self-styled guru) who is really just running a business. {{t:00:01:31}}
  • The actual signal to catch: if you are trying to do something and keep hitting frustration, that itself is the sign that something, or someone, is holding you back a little. If life gently stops or delays you, read it as a cue to wait rather than force it. {{t:00:02:04}}
  • Until your own mind's "speed" slows down, you stay stuck in a large whirlpool of frustration, and people spend years, even decades, caught in it without knowing why, eventually drifting into superstition instead of understanding the actual cause. {{t:00:02:35}}

That frustration loop is the speaker's reason for today's class: gemstones worn for the planets, and the ishta devta (personal deity) that is set for each person. {{t:00:02:35}}

2. Bhagwan, Ishwar, Ishta and Guru {{t:00:03:05}}

TermWhat the speaker means by it
BhagwanEarth, sky, air, fire and water combined through the physical act of union: the body itself, as matter. {{t:00:03:05}}
IshwarThe "var" (groom) of Ish: the one accepted and settled upon, the way a bride and groom accept each other. {{t:00:03:38}}
IshtaYour specific deity, found the way a doctor finds a specific deficiency from a blood test: magnesium, sodium, calcium, whichever it is. In the same way your life has one specific deity, whether Ram, Hanuman or Shiva, that is actually yours. {{t:00:03:38}}
GuruSomeone who already has clarity and can give it to you, the way a doctor tells you what food suits your body and what does not. {{t:00:07:50}}

Other religions simplify this into one word, "God" or "Allah." The speaker ties the Sikh teaching of "Ek Onkar" (the One, formless, without an image) to the same idea of the formless, and reads it as Guru Nanak's advice to stop fighting over which form to worship and instead follow a guru. {{t:00:05:14}}

3. Stuck chakras, and the milk-to-ghee refining process {{t:00:08:52}}

  • The two lowest chakras, muladhara (root) and swadhisthana (sacral), are self-activated and are where someone gets stuck saying "I won't listen to anyone," an attitude the speaker ties to certain planetary placements rather than simple stubbornness. {{t:00:08:52}}
  • Until a person rises to the navel chakra, where he places Mars, Jupiter and Saturn in this scheme, they will not develop the intellectual clarity to know what they actually should be doing, and stay confused and anxious instead. {{t:00:09:24}}
  • His milk-to-ghee analogy for being "beaten into shape." Milk churned hard enough separates into water, curd, buttermilk and finally ghee; a person who has been knocked around enough by life, in his telling, becomes refined the same way, and once the refinement goes far enough, that person starts acting as a guide for others. Ghee, unlike milk, does not spoil if a fly lands in it, you simply remove the fly; milk left open for a day is ruined entirely. {{t:00:09:56}}
  • He extends this to a home's own vastu (layout): just as a building needs its own kind of doctor to check it, this depth of worship and chart-reading needs its own kind of doctor too, which is the role he assigns to a qualified astrologer. {{t:00:11:01}}
