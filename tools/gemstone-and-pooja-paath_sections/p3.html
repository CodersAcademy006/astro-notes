5. The planetary family: who serves whom {{t:00:21:22}}

To explain how the nine planets relate to each other, the speaker tells an extended family story: Suryakant (the Sun), his wife Chandrakanta (the Moon), their son Mangal (Mars), their daughter Budhi (Mercury), the family's loyal servant Shannu (Saturn), a suitor named Shakku (Venus), and Rahu and Ketu as Shannu's own helpers. {{t:00:22:26}}

  • Surya and Chandra (Sun and Moon). Husband and wife, never enemies, taking turns the way day follows night: "I arrive, you leave; I move, you move." All light and life on Earth traces back to this pair. {{t:00:24:01}}
  • Mangal (Mars), the son. Born from the couple, loved without condition. Mars and the Sun are never enemies, father and son, whatever else happens. {{t:00:25:38}}
  • Guru (Jupiter), the honoured guest. The one planet whose gravity holds the solar system together, pulling Mercury and Mars back from falling into the Sun while Saturn and the outer planets pull them the other way. In the family story, the Sun treats Jupiter with total warmth, and Jupiter is never unkind to the son. When Jupiter and the Moon sit together in a birth chart, it is called gaj kesari yoga, said to give the highest results. {{t:00:28:42}}
  • Budhi (Mercury), the daughter. Quarrels constantly with her brother Mangal, the ordinary sibling friction, never outright enmity. The father dotes on her the way fathers favour daughters, and the mother leans toward the son, a pattern the speaker calls natural rather than fair. {{t:00:26:40}}
  • Shannu (Saturn), the servant. Serves the whole household without question, terrified of the Sun, and never dares step out of line in front of the family's actual master. {{t:00:28:42}}
  • Shakku (Venus), the suitor. Hopes to marry into the family and become its mistress. Deep friendship with Saturn and Mercury, outright enmity with Jupiter, and complete fear of the Sun. The mother privately cannot stand her but can do nothing because of how useful her service is. {{t:00:35:00}}
  • Rahu and Ketu. Shannu's own subordinates, loyal to him and, through him, to the household. {{t:00:37:05}}
  • When the guru first visits the household in the story, Chandrakanta greets him with folded hands and full devotion, a detail the speaker uses to show how completely the Moon submits to Jupiter's guidance. {{t:00:23:31}}
  • Venus (Shakku) goes weak and silent the instant the Sun is near, in his telling, however bold she is with everyone else, which is his way of showing just how total the Sun's authority is over her. {{t:00:36:33}}

Why this matters for worship: each planet's relationships decide which deity's charity or prayer helps another planet and which one damages it. The speaker's running example is giving Saturn's items (oil, iron) into a chart where Mars sits strong: oil poured next to fire, in his words, only makes the fire worse rather than calming anything. {{t:00:54:23}}
