9. If saints had such a strong energy body, why were so many gravely ill? {{t:01:27:40}}

A detailed question asks why Ramakrishna Paramhansa, Swami Vivekananda and Ramana Maharshi, whose spiritual energy was said to be at its peak, still suffered severe physical illness and aged quickly. The speaker's answer below is his own theological opinion about named historical religious figures, offered respectfully; these notes do not take a position on it.

  • His core answer: there is no contradiction, because the energy body and the physical body are not automatically in sync just because the energy body is "high"; in his telling, each of these figures fought their own body rather than living in ease with it. {{t:01:28:11}}
  • His example of Vivekananda: a story in which he refused to even look at a dancer performing nearby, out of fear that watching her would stir desire in him; the speaker reads this as suppression (hatha yoga, forced effort) rather than the easier, natural path (sahaj yoga) described elsewhere in this series. {{t:01:28:42}}
  • His example of Ramakrishna Paramhansa: an account, which he flags himself as unverified, that worshipping in a feminine form caused him to menstruate; the speaker reads this as evidence of how hard he fought against his own body rather than accepting it. {{t:01:29:13}}
  • His reading of Ramana Maharshi's final illness: told his attendants not to try to save him, since he had decided it was his time; the speaker treats this as a case of someone no longer "loving" or holding on to the body at all, in contrast to people who cling to life and refuse to let go even in severe decline. {{t:01:30:17}}

He argues for the Buddhist-style middle way over either extreme, neither full indulgence nor full renunciation, as what keeps a person balanced; going to either extreme (he includes suicide born of over-indulgence, and self-denial born of excess austerity, as the two failure modes) is itself the imbalance that causes harm. {{t:01:29:45}}
  • He adds that a body is "abandoned" (and more vulnerable to illness, in his claim, including cancer) when a person stops loving or caring for it at all, whichever direction that neglect comes from. {{t:01:29:45}}
