7. Sun dosha at home, and the remedies he gives {{t:00:54:14}}

Signs of a disturbed Sun

Cracked plaster or flooring, chipped stone, red or brown ants swarming inside the house, broken wooden furniture or photo frames, especially anything belonging to or used by the household's elder men. {{t:00:54:14}}

Why it matters

He ties a weak Sun directly to a lack of respect for elders: disrespecting them (even just failing the small daily courtesies) weakens your own Sun, your energy, bones, eyesight and public standing. {{t:00:55:15}}

What he recommends

Repair cracks and broken furniture immediately, reduce added table salt, keep a light on in any room the Sun's light can't reach, and touch the feet of parents and elders regularly, even if you privately resent them. {{t:00:57:51}}

  • A weak Sun, he claims, shows up as rising blood pressure, cholesterol trouble, heart issues, weak bones, eye trouble, joint pain, uric acid problems, kidney or gallbladder stones, and failing eyesight, roughly in that order of likelihood. {{t:00:57:51}}
  • He links a dim household (heavy curtains, blacked-out car windows, rooms kept dark out of habit) directly to a suppressed Sun, separate from any chart placement. {{t:00:56:46}}
  • Helping others when you can, he says, is the single biggest general remedy for a negative Sun; he adds that government-related work and approvals specifically run on the Sun's grace, so a stuck government matter reads as a Sun problem. {{t:00:56:16}}
  • Salt and the Sun. He treats salt itself, sea salt especially, as pure Sun; the general rule he gives is to season food while cooking rather than adding raw salt at the table, and to use black salt or pink/rock salt as an alternative when something feels "too salty" rather than adding more plain salt on top. He is clear that no particular salt (pink, rock, sea) is nutritionally special, since the sodium is the same either way. {{t:00:58:22}}
  • He distinguishes rock salt from sea salt by origin: rock salt comes from stone, which he reads as carrying a touch of Mars alongside the Sun, while sea salt is "pure" Sun with no Mars mixed in. {{t:01:00:27}}
  • Touching elders' feet. His instructions: right hand on the right foot, left on left, hands then brought to the forehead. He suggests doing this even toward difficult or harmful parents, treating it as training for the body rather than a verdict on the relationship; if your actual parents are not workable, he suggests symbolically "adopting" substitute parent figures and extending the same gesture to them. {{t:01:02:03}}
  • He claims that touching elders' feet with real feeling, even while silently still angry, gradually reshapes a person's habits and temperament until the anger itself fades without effort. {{t:01:03:35}}
  • Posture matters in his scheme too: bowing lowers the usual "ego posture" of the neck pulled back, and he links regularly bending the spine forward (as in a bow) to quietly weakening ego over time. {{t:01:03:35}}
  • His mechanism for ego. He describes ego (ahankar) as the mind "grabbing hold of" the body for its own impulses; a body that does not instantly obey every craving the mind raises, for sweets, for impulsive spending, is, in his claim, what gradually loosens that grip. {{t:01:04:07}}
  • Red or brown ants swarming inside a house are, in his claim, a direct Sun-dosha sign on their own, separate from cracked plaster or broken furniture. {{t:01:06:18}}
  • He links "the evil eye" (nazar lagna) to a weakened aura from a suppressed Sun, rather than treating it as a separate, unrelated superstition. {{t:01:07:22}}

Associating specific illnesses (blood pressure, cholesterol, stones, eyesight) directly with a disturbed Sun or with disrespecting elders is the speaker's own belief system, not a medical claim. These notes do not endorse changing medical care on this basis.

8. Why pray at all? Ritual as a kind of hypnosis {{t:01:09:27}}

A long audience thread asks the speaker to justify ritual itself: is daily puja, a lit lamp, a bell, actually doing anything, or is it only habit?

The question

If the "cosmic body" doing the real work has nothing to do with physical food, timing or custom, why keep any ritual at all, and what is the right way to do it? {{t:01:13:36}}

His answer

Most ritual is a form of fear, not science: people fear losing what they have, or are quietly begging for more. He is blunt that this is a form of hypnosis most people never examine. {{t:01:08:26}}

His recommendation

Ask yourself honestly why you perform a ritual before doing it. If you know your Ishta and the exact science behind it, connect precisely; if you don't, pick whichever practice gives you real peace and stay with that, rather than performing out of inherited fear. {{t:01:08:26}}

  • Prayer does not replace practical action. His example: soldiers may pray out of custom before a battle, then still go and ask for a weapon and ammunition, not more prayer; a surgeon operates holding a scalpel, not a prayer bead, in his hand. Ritual, in his telling, is not a substitute for the practical step actually required. {{t:01:08:57}}
  • Why the same food affects people differently. One family member reacts badly to sugar while another eats the same amount with no issue; he reads this as each body's own system (shaped by past-life karma, prarabdh) automatically handling things differently, not God singling anyone out for reward or punishment. {{t:01:10:30}}
  • The "flip the switch" analogy. His central image: everyone chanting, bowing or praying in their own tradition is really just trying to flip the same cosmic light switch. Some people learn there is a direct switch and flip it straight away; others need a longer ritual route to reach the same switch. Neither is more "spiritually advanced" except in how directly they get there. {{t:01:18:19}}
  • He offers a rough, unverified figure that roughly 500 million people in India practice some form of puja, out of a global population of 8 to 9 billion, framing devotional ritual as one option among many equally valid traditions worldwide. {{t:01:14:39}}
  • Repetitive chanting (naming a popular mantra as his example), in his claim, works mainly by keeping the mind (munna) too busy to generate its usual stray, troubling thoughts, not through the specific words themselves. {{t:01:19:52}}
  • He pushes back on relying on chanting alone for practical outcomes: war, earning money or falling ill will still follow their own course regardless of how much a name is repeated; the chant is a tool for steadying the mind, not a universal override on events. {{t:01:20:22}}
  • He is skeptical of ritual performed only from fear: "why keep God around just to be scared of him?" and contrasts this with the more transactional, confident attitude he associates with a strong Sun (covered in the Sun's own section above). {{t:01:07:53}}
  • He singles out one community, mentioning Jain practitioners who keep strict dietary discipline while running large, highly profitable enterprises, as evidence that strict ritual observance and worldly wealth are not mutually exclusive, and are not proof either way of inner spiritual progress. {{t:01:21:25}}
  • He contrasts figures who accumulate wealth (Lakshmi, in his terms) against ones who accumulate reputation for wisdom and devotional writing instead (Saraswati), using poet-saints as his examples of the second path, without claiming one is superior. {{t:01:23:00}}
  • He is blunt that outcomes are not decided by divine favouritism: "God hasn't opened a soda-water shop" handing out fortune or hardship by whim; in his telling, a person's own karma decides what they get, whoever they are. {{t:01:12:32}}
  • He frames the cosmos itself as still expanding from a single origin point (he references the scientific idea of a single starting point roughly 4.5 billion years ago), and suggests human understanding keeps expanding in step with it, so no single tradition's current explanation is likely to be the final word. {{t:01:17:16}}
  • His test for whether a practice or an assumed Ishta is right for you: not a theory, but whether your actual life gets easier over time: "life itself is the litmus test." {{t:01:26:07}}
