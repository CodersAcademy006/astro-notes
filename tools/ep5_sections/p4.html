7. Do not share jhoota: how one body's leftovers carry another's vibration {{t:00:25:52}}

Before giving the detailed gift rules, the speaker lays down a rule he treats as basic hygiene for planetary vibration: never eat or drink after someone else, and be careful whose "jhoota" (what is left after someone has already bitten, drunk or touched it with their mouth) touches you. {{t:00:25:52}} {{t:00:26:24}}

The claim

Whatever a person's state of mind, health or habits, that vibration is said to travel through their saliva and touch, into whatever leftover food, drink or clothing passes to you. {{t:00:27:58}} {{t:00:28:30}}

His examples

He compares it to hospital wards that separate patients by condition and give separate toilets to kidney and liver patients, and to small children being told to cover themselves and stay hidden from strangers' eyes, as proof that "vibration hygiene" is already half-believed by everyone. {{t:00:31:06}} {{t:00:31:37}}

His rule

Eat with the right hand; use the left hand only to serve or handle other items, so your own "touched" hand never contaminates the common dish. Only share jhoota with people you intend to spend your life vibration with, such as a spouse, deliberately. {{t:00:26:24}} {{t:00:29:31}} {{t:00:32:07}}

  • A sadhu or saint, in his account, never eats anyone's leftovers and never lets anyone eat his, which is why their "chakras" are said to stay undisturbed. {{t:00:30:03}} {{t:00:30:35}}
  • He draws a line at puberty: once a daughter has started her periods, or a son is producing semen, elders should stop taking their jhoota, since the child's own sexual energy is now active and the vibration exchange changes meaning. {{t:00:32:38}} {{t:00:33:08}}
  • He says this is not about culture or politeness: eating from one tiffin with many hands, or feeding each other at a wedding, is in his view an unknowing exchange of planetary vibration between people, not a harmless custom. {{t:00:29:31}} {{t:00:30:03}}

The claim that shared food or touch transfers planetary vibration, the hospital and children's hygiene comparisons, and the rule about a child past puberty are the speaker's personal belief. They are not a medical or public-health claim, and nothing here should be read as advice about hygiene, infection control or a child's body.
