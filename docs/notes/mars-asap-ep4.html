His answer

Manifestation, in his framework, does not override destiny; it refines or improves the outcome within what is already set, the same way a person can still choose how well they walk a path whose destination is fixed. 02:11:13

  • His reading of Ram's exile despite a court astrologer's favourable timing for his coronation: Ram is held up as the model of accepting events without judging them as good or bad, which the speaker calls "leela" (divine play) rather than a failure of the astrologer's calculation. 02:17:00
  • He distinguishes two kinds of "knowledge" in this closing discussion: consciously choosing an outcome (collapsing the wave into a particle, in his recurring quantum analogy), versus consciously allowing events to unfold without interference (staying in superposition, his term for the detached, "whatever happens, happens" stance); he treats both as equally valid forms of wisdom rather than ranking one above the other. 02:18:33
