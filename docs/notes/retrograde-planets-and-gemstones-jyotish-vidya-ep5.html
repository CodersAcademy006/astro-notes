The talk in brief

  1. Comparison switches you retrograde. Dwelling on someone else's pace can hand power to your own natal retrograde planet.
  2. Vakri never truly reverses. It only looks that way next to something moving faster, like a slower walker in a park.
  3. A retrograde planet needs its own stone. His universal law: fit a clean, well-matched gemstone for whichever planet is retrograde at birth.
  4. The stone filters, it does not boost. A gem never raises or lowers a planet's power, it only strains how much of it reaches you.
  5. Do not share jhoota. Leftover food, drink or clothing is said to carry another person's planetary vibration.
  6. Gifts can switch a house's dosha on. What you hand your spouse, by colour and item, is read against the sign sitting in the 7th house.
  7. Each planet matures at its own age. Moon at 5 through Ketu at 48; a retrograde dosha is said to start biting only after that age.

3. What "vakri" really means: Mona and Minal in the park 00:13:42

The speaker's core teaching example: a clock always ticks forward, yet we sometimes say our life feels like it is running backward. He uses two people walking in a park to show what a retrograde planet actually is. 00:09:56 00:13:42

The picture

Mona and Minal start walking together. Mona spots a cousin in the distance and races ahead. Minal, picking up something Mona dropped, keeps walking slowly in the same direction. 00:13:42 00:14:15

The illusion

Both are still walking forward the whole time. But because Mona is moving so much faster, from Mona's point of view Minal looks like she is falling behind, drifting backward, even though Minal never once reversed direction. 00:14:46 00:15:18

His definition

"Vakri" (retrograde) is exactly this look of going backward. A retrograde planet never truly reverses in space. It only looks that way against a faster-moving reference point such as the Earth. 00:09:56 00:15:50

  • Saturn takes about 30 years to go once around the Sun, Jupiter about 12. The Earth, going around in a single year, is far faster than either, so at certain points in the Earth's orbit, Jupiter or Saturn can appear to crawl backward against the stars. 00:15:50 00:16:23
  • His business example: one person earns a crore (10 million rupees) in two years, another takes five years for the same amount. People call the slower one "stuck", "retrograde", comparing him with the faster one, even though the slower person is still moving ahead in his own life. 00:16:55 00:17:27
  • He folds this back into the opening point: a person's own brain can run the same "vakri" trick, pulling attention backward into comparison with someone else's pace, rather than their own. 00:17:27 00:18:01
