The talk in brief

  1. Kripa (grace) decides more than luck. Whether you find the right doctor, astrologer or partner depends on grace from your Ishta, not chance.
  2. An exalted planet can make you your own Ishta. When your own planet already sits strong in the right house, you may not need to worship anything outside yourself.
  3. The 9th house sign suggests your starting Ishta. A simple table maps each sign to a planet and a deity, but lagna, bhava chalit and navamsa can each point somewhere different.
  4. Worshipping a weak planet can backfire. Puja activates a planet's energy, so activating an already debilitated placement can make things worse, not better.
  5. Mars and marriage are tied through fire. The wedding pheras ignite a manglik Mars; skip the fire with a court marriage or Anand Karaj and the dosha has nothing to activate.
  6. Mercury and Jupiter retrograde both got a preview. Retrograde Mercury cannot stay quiet, retrograde Jupiter inflates the ego around age 15 to 16; the full class continues in Ep. 5.
  7. The remedy behind most of this is simply not reacting. Grace, karma and planetary fixes all point back to the same habit: do not give your body to someone else's provocation.

3. Quick recap: fixed houses, moving signs 00:12:30

Before the worked examples, a fast refresher on why a birth chart looks the way it does. 00:12:30

  • A birth chart (janma kundali) has 12 bhavas (houses), numbered 1 to 12 in a fixed ring. 00:12:30
  • Houses map to fixed body parts: house 1 is always the head, and so on round the chart; a mole, injury or illness can land in different signs for different people, but the house-to-body mapping itself never moves. 00:13:03
  • What does move is which rashi (zodiac sign) sits in which house, because the zodiac wheel itself rotates. 00:13:34
  • Why the zodiac seems to run backwards. Earth spins anticlockwise on its own axis while Venus spins clockwise; because we are standing on an anticlockwise-spinning Earth, the signs appear to move in reverse across the sky over a day. 00:14:06
  • The Sun's sign changes only about once a month and is the same for everyone born in that month, so the sign that actually matters for an individual chart is whichever one sits in the ascendant (lagna) the moment they are born. 00:15:41
  • Other planets change sign at very different speeds: the Moon roughly every two and a half days, Jupiter over about 12 to 13 months, Mars anywhere from a few months up, Rahu about every one and a half years, Venus about every one and a quarter months, Saturn about two and a half years in a sign (30 years for a full cycle), Mercury roughly a month, staying close to the Sun. 00:16:11
  • Practically, nobody needs to track this by hand: chart software plots it. The skill worth learning is reading a finished chart, not calculating planetary motion. 00:16:42
  • Sade-sati, explained simply. It is just Saturn's two-and-a-half-year transit through the sign before your Moon sign, through your Moon sign itself, and through the sign after, a seven-and-a-half year window where Saturn sits closest to your Moon. 00:17:14
  • Once a person's own placements are "settled" (tik gaye), outside opinion stops moving them. Ram and Ravana shared a sign; so did Krishna and Kansa; blaming Saturn alone for a bad run misses that the rest of the chart, and the person's own consciousness, decide the outcome. 00:17:47
