The talk in brief

  1. Knowledge has a ceiling, wisdom does not. A guru is a guide to the next stretch of road, not a place to stop.
  2. A king does not beg. Ask from a sense of right, not from helplessness, to keep the Sun strong.
  3. Every planet gets a consciousness fix. Listening settles Rahu, feeding a dog settles Ketu, tidy storage settles Jupiter, and so on through all nine.
  4. Mercury and Venus sit on a seesaw. A level pair gives an ordinary mix of luck; a tipped pair swings between extremes.
  5. A gemstone only limits downside. It is damage control, not a source of success that was not already due.
  6. A fixed share of life still plays out. The speaker puts it at about 12 percent, regardless of status or faith.
  7. The closing chart reading shows the method. Retrograde and combust planets, and a house ruler's seat elsewhere in the chart, decide how a dosha plays out.

1. Knowledge, wisdom and the first lesson on consciousness 00:00:41

This is the opening class of a new series the speaker calls ASAP, built around consciousness (chetana) rather than remedies alone. His starting claim: a person's awareness of their own life can move a chart's results, and the class will spend the rest of the talk showing this planet by planet.

Knowledge (नॉलेज)

Comes from books and outside sources. It has a ceiling: you can finish learning a subject. 00:00:41

Wisdom (ज्ञान)

Rises from inside a person and has no limit. The speaker says every year of his own life he still feels he knows nothing yet. 00:00:41

The guru's job

A guru is only a guide to the next stretch of road, not a stopping point. Stopping at a guru, a temple, an idol, a daughter-in-law or a spouse keeps the same grief (klesh) running. 00:01:12

  • The speaker's own framing: do your karma and let "the owner" (Vishnu) resolve the rest, since Vishnu does all the acting and Lakshmi stays at his feet. Money is important: those who say money is nothing usually have plenty of it, and those who worship poverty usually have too much lying idle. 00:01:43
  • He suggests working for money honestly until the urge to give some away arises on its own, rather than assuming you already have enough. 00:02:46
  • His picture of the soul's journey: we get stuck at a guru, a temple or a person for lifetimes, and each time we start to let go, "God or munna" hands us a fresh grief, story or tangle to stay stuck on. 00:02:14
  • Early success is a trap. His example: a business or relationship that succeeds too easily early on sets a benchmark that nothing later can beat, so later, larger success stops giving any real pleasure. He suggests welcoming early failure instead, as protection against this trap. 00:04:18
  • He reads being "beaten" or humbled early in life as a kind of initiation, comparing it to Arjuna's despair before receiving Krishna's teaching: knowledge, in his telling, is not handed out freely but after hardship. 00:04:50
  • His definition of "chaitanya" (awakened consciousness). Simply knowing what you are: a child who knows he is a child is a "wise child", and a woman who knows she is a woman is a "wise woman". Believing yourself special, divine or above the rules, in his view, is not elevated consciousness but a different problem altogether. 00:03:47

2. The Sun: a king does not beg 00:06:22

The speaker opens the planet-by-planet tour with the Sun, built around one behaviour: begging versus owning your right (hak) to ask.

The pattern

Someone who is always begging, from God, from a bank clerk, from a relative, is acting like a subject, not a king. A king bows to God with gratitude, not demands. 00:06:22

Why it is Sun

The Sun is "our God", the centre that keeps the solar system alive and the giver of unlimited, continuous light (photons). A weak Sun in the chart, he says, shows up in people who spend their life begging. 00:06:22

Consciousness shift

Recognise you have a right (hak) to ask, the way a child assumes a right over a parent. Dropping the sin/virtue duality (dvait) in your own head is, in his framing, what keeps the Sun strong. 00:07:58

  • He places the Sun's East direction (E5 in his own shorthand) at the house's east side, tied to the honour of the home's elders. 00:07:27
  • Everyone who begs through religious ritual all their life, in his reading, has a troubled Sun; the fault is not about one incident but a running pattern. 00:07:27
  • Duality (dvait). He claims people who stop dividing everything into "good" and "bad" rise to the top and keep a Sun-type confidence, giving a currently prominent world leader in his eighties as his example of someone, in his view, free of that duality. 00:08:30
  • Right eye is Sun, linked to the pituitary gland; left eye is Moon, linked to the pineal gland. Together he calls this pair "Goloka Dham". 00:10:34
  • A person who scolds someone and then regrets it, in his framing, always has a weak Sun, and it can weaken the right eye (or the left, in a baby born breech). 00:10:34
  • Street beggars, in his view, are all playing out a Saturn or Rahu role, never a Sun role, in their chart. 00:11:06
  • Bhiksha versus bheekh. Asked whether monks and renunciates who ask for alms (bhiksha) are different from beggars (bheekh), he says yes: bhiksha is asked for as a right (dharma), and a true renunciate does not curse those who refuse, while a beggar does. 00:13:46
  • He adds that acceptance (not mere adjustment) of life as it comes is what makes someone "a king" in this sense; whoever cannot accept family, work or circumstance ends up with a ruined Sun and a growing sense of helplessness. 00:14:17
  • He distinguishes real confidence from ego by whether a person is even aware their confidence might look like arrogance and still proceeds without flinching; once that awareness is present, he claims, it no longer functions as ego at all. 00:09:01

The speaker names a living world leader as his example of someone beyond "good and bad" thinking, and separately references other current political figures and events. These are his personal political opinions stated inside an astrology class, not astrological analysis, and are not repeated by name in these notes.

4. Mars: friendship is the medicine 00:16:56

Why it is Mars

  • Easing Mars, he claims, also eases blood-pressure complaints linked to the stress of constant conflict. 00:20:34
  • Giving advice "like a brother" before friendship is established gets rejected; once trust (a Mars-fixed bond) is there, the same advice lands. 00:20:34
  • If a friend betrays you and you retaliate in kind, he says you damage your own Mars; letting it go lets Mars settle the account on its own, often by sending a new ally. 00:21:06

  • Confidence itself, he claims, is Mars's grace; a confident person who has not wronged anyone has nothing to fear from Saturn or Rahu either. 00:22:44
  • Teasing is not disrespect. Banter and mock insults between close friends, he says, are Mars (affection), not a Jupiter offence; a comedy show built on friends insulting each other works the same way, since the feeling behind it is love, not contempt. 00:37:22

5. Mercury: the empty space and the Mercury-Venus balance 00:23:14

Mercury, in this talk, is less about communication and more about empty space and the balance of a life, paired on a seesaw with Venus.

The pattern

Nothing in life feels balanced: good luck and bad luck don't average out, a plant won't grow when you plant it, a cow won't eat green fodder from your hand, cobwebs collect in the house. 00:23:47

Why it is Mercury

Mercury is the open sky/empty space a person moves through, "almost everyone's friend", neutral-gendered, and it checks whether the air passages of a house or a life are clear. 00:24:20

Consciousness shift

On one side of the seesaw sits Mercury, on the other Venus (enjoyment); a level seesaw means a life with a workable mix of good and bad, neither chasing extreme discipline nor extreme indulgence. 00:24:53

  • If your life keeps swinging between extremes with no evening-out, good job but no peace, or endless bad luck with no relief, he reads it as the Mercury-Venus seesaw being broken, whether the imbalance is in job, work or money. 00:24:53
  • His test for this fault. Whether a plant lives when you plant it by hand, and whether a cow will take green fodder from your hand. If neither happens, he reads the "aura" (and the person's magnetism, which he assigns to Rahu) as weak, which blocks intuition. 00:26:25
  • His image for Mercury: the empty space inside a glass, not the glass itself; what fills that space, in a glass, a house or a life, is what Mercury's balance is really checking. 00:23:14
  • Cobwebs in the house are, in his claim, a sign of blocked air passages (Mercury's domain); a spider itself is not the problem, only the web being allowed to sit. 00:26:25
  • A chronic shortfall or surplus with no explanation, bills that never add up, money that "just disappears", is his example of a Mercury-driven imbalance rather than a Venus one. 00:28:30

The speaker tells a story about a government placing a lemon under a fighter jet's tyre as a protective folk custom, as an example of popular superstition he considers harmless "consciousness management" for the public. 6. Jupiter: respect, broken idols and the store-room 00:29:32

The pattern

Damaged books, a broken idol kept around out of sentiment, a north-east storage cupboard crammed with junk, torn clothes left unmended, or casually abusing people without reason. 00:29:32

Why it is Jupiter

Jupiter is the "king" of the solar system, the force that, in his model, keeps the Sun's gravity from swallowing the other planets, and the north-east is its zone. Keeping that zone cluttered, or disrespecting religious objects and gurus, unsettles Jupiter and, he claims, tanks a person's success rate. 00:33:42

Consciousness shift

Keep things orderly and mend what's torn (a stitch, a patch) instead of leaving damage to sit. Respect flows from inner humility, not performance; even a hostile Jupiter-Rahu combination (the classically "infamous" guru-chandal yoga) can, in his reading, turn a difficult person wise. 00:33:09

  • A broken idol or torn religious book should simply be discarded with basic respect, not kept "because it's still sacred"; keeping it damaged is the dosha. 00:31:06
  • His image for that discarding: like a funeral, the item's usefulness has ended, not its worth, so disposing of it respectfully, not necessarily with ceremony, is the point rather than an insult. 00:52:22
  • He also pictures a king bowing first to his own guru (rajguru), presenting the guru's authority as higher than royal status in his hierarchy of respect. 00:30:34
  • A cluttered north-east storage cupboard is treated as an almost universal trap: he says no one builds a cupboard without eventually shoving a screwdriver or stray tool into it. 00:31:37
  • Getting genuine respect from others (versus being waved off) is, in his claim, a direct readout of how strong your Jupiter is. 00:35:47
  • He pictures Jupiter as the solar system's great magnet, strong enough that, in his telling, even a hostile "chandal" (outcast) can be made wise once Jupiter holds Rahu rather than the reverse. 00:33:42
  • A torn piece of clothing, a cracked wall, crumbling plaster or peeling paint at home are all, in his scheme, variations of the same Jupiter-and-Rahu disturbance, and all call for the same basic response: repair it rather than ignore it. 00:34:14
  • Discarded old clothes should still be mended and given cleanly, not handed over torn; giving a damaged item to a household helper, he says, "gives Saturn to Saturn" and the debt comes back. 00:49:46
  • He recommends all giving be done quietly, without naming the amount or day, so "the planet involved never gets to measure it" and no dosha attaches. 00:51:51
  • He extends the same rule to religious donations: if someone asks for a specific amount or item by name, he suggests giving through a different, unnamed channel instead, so the gift still stays unmeasured. 00:53:55
  • Rusted iron, worn-out door hinges that creak, and a worn doormat are, in his scheme, Saturn's warning signs rather than Jupiter's; he says mend or replace them before they fully break. 00:49:46

8. Rahu: a head that cannot act 00:55:57

Rahu, in this talk, is framed around helplessness and not listening: Rahu can see, speak and hear, but cannot act on its own.

The pattern

A running sense of helplessness: stuck in love, stuck on a visa, stuck in a job interview, and underneath it, a habit of not listening to anyone. 00:56:33

Why it is Rahu

He frames it as a cycle: refusing to listen starts it, helplessness ends it. Rahu is also a great observer and witness (sakshi bhav); hearing out others, even in a quarrel, is what keeps Rahu in its useful, knowledge-gathering mode instead of its "can't act" mode. 00:57:34

Consciousness shift

Be a listener. He claims genuinely listening (not just waiting to speak) strengthens Rahu, while listening to "useless talk" strengthens its negative side instead. 00:59:08

  • In-law conflict as Rahu's home. He reads in-law disputes, and the habit of trading escalating complaints ("my grievance is bigger than yours"), as classic Rahu: nobody is actually listening. Learning to stay quiet in that exact situation is, in his claim, Rahu settling. 00:57:03
  • During a Rahu dasha specifically, he says the person cannot get work done by their own hands and should delegate, hiring an assistant rather than fighting the helplessness directly. 00:59:38
  • Blue is Rahu's colour, tied to how far the eye can see into the ocean; he links a Rahu dasha to more delegation in general. 00:59:38
  • Overthinking and looping, anxious thought, he assigns to Rahu "eating" the Moon (an eclipse of the mind), separate from Rahu's own extreme, which he calls a pull toward self-harm rather than looping thought. 01:02:16
  • Samudra manthan as inner imagery. He reads the churning-of-the-ocean myth as a picture of the body's own energy channels (ida and pingala nadi) churning around the base chakra, not a literal ocean. 01:03:17
  • He assigns the body's nectar (amrit) to a cycle guarded on one side by Rahu, pushing it up and out, and on the other by the Moon, which is tasked with keeping it inside the body; he treats this as his own symbolic reading of the churning myth rather than literal physiology. 01:00:09
  • Delegating work to others, he says, is the single clearest marker of a Rahu dasha in progress, whatever the person's actual role or seniority. 00:59:38
  • Simply staying silent in a moment of conflict, separate from genuinely listening, is itself enough to settle Rahu, he claims. 00:58:37
  • He separates helplessness (bebasi) itself from giving up; true surrender, he claims, only follows once a person stops ranking themselves against others altogether. 01:00:39
  • He describes three stages of ego: believing yourself a god ("the first"), accepting you are one among many ordinary people ("the second"), and truly accepting you are "a distant third" with no special claim. Real surrender, in his account, only happens at that third stage. 01:01:11

The reading of Rahu's extreme state as suicidal despair is the speaker's own framing inside a mythological discussion; it is not clinical and should not be read as mental-health guidance. Readers in distress should seek a qualified professional.

9. Ketu: the dog, the fakir and the lower body 01:03:47

The pattern

A dog won't eat from your hand, or barks at and troubles you; skin disease; back, tailbone or lower-body pain; a bitten tongue; broken bins or footwear at home. 01:06:57

Why it is Ketu

Ketu is the headless body: it can act but not judge, pictured as a dog on a leash following wherever the "head" (a partner, friend or leader) pulls. It governs everything below the neck, the tongue, skin, and the capacity to enjoy anything bodily. 01:06:26

Consciousness shift

Feed a dog; it is, in his claim, the simplest way to please Ketu. He widens this to feeding cows, crows, wandering fakirs and saints, and setting aside a guest's share before eating, though he does not detail that remedy fully in this talk. 01:07:58

  • Who counts as a "fakir" or saint. Not someone in a particular costume, but someone who has stopped begging and says "that's enough" when given something; he calls this "pure Ketu". Robes and ritual appearance, in his view, are theatre and not the substance of sainthood. 01:08:30
  • A small accident that leaves you unhurt, he reads as Ketu's grace; a severe accident with only a scratch is the same grace at a larger scale. A bitten tongue signals the exact moment Ketu is disturbed, worth a quick bow to the dog or to Bhairav (Ketu's deity, linked to the south-west). 01:07:27
  • His mythological anchor for the dog image: the same loyal, headless dog later walks with Yudhishthira all the way to heaven, which he reads as Ketu faithfully following a righteous head to the very end. 01:07:21
  • Eating in bed is fine in itself; what unsettles Ketu, in his claim, is when a bed used for intimacy is also used as a dining table, since the two functions interfere with each other by his account. 01:09:33
  • If a married child still will not treat you as a parent, or a daughter never fully "becomes" a daughter in her manner, he reads that as a Mercury or Jupiter fault rather than Ketu, since Ketu's own domain stops at the lower body and its enjoyment. 01:10:04
  • Discarding a broken dustbin is, in his claim, fine and even useful: a bin itself is linked to Ketu, so a damaged one is best thrown out rather than kept in use. 01:09:33
  • A skin complaint, a mouth ulcer or a heated, irritable stomach are all, in his scheme, Ketu disturbances; he separates this from the sexual and digestive functions Ketu also governs. 01:09:33
  • He widens "guru" here to mean whoever first triggers a real shift in a person's awareness, whether that is a formal teacher, a sibling, a parent, or even a daughter-in-law. 01:05:22
  • Specific dietary customs tied to a day of the week, he says, apply only to someone whose own Venus is already compromised; he claims a person with a clean Venus placement can eat something others are warned against without the same consequence. 01:11:06

Associating meat-eating customs or specific diets with planetary doshas is the speaker's personal belief framework. These notes do not endorse or discourage any dietary or religious practice on that basis.

10. Indra, Neptune and Pluto: enjoyment, grace and death 01:12:39

Past the nine classical grahas, the speaker folds in three more figures from his own framework: Indra for enjoyment, Neptune for grace, and Pluto for death and chronic illness.

Indra: enjoyment blocked

A dirty, cluttered house; money sitting in the account while the person still travels by the cheapest option out of habit; never actually using what you've earned. He claims this is also a leading cause he associates with cancer, through chronic clutter in the home's south, south-west and north-east zones. 01:12:39

Neptune: grace missing

Neither medicine nor alcohol seems to "work" on the person, in the folk phrase he quotes: "medicine won't touch him, try liquor instead." That double failure, in his claim, signals someone with no grace (kripa) left to draw on. 01:17:52

Pluto: illness and fear

A string of small, recurring health troubles that never clear up fully is, in his claim, Pluto unsettled; he calls Pluto the "lord of death" and ties it to the chronic, slow kind of suffering rather than a single acute illness. 01:21:33

  • Indra's fix: use what you have. He says the remedy is simply to spend on comfort you can afford instead of hoarding out of fear, and to keep the house clean, especially wardrobes and cupboards nobody opens. 01:14:14
  • Neptune's fix: "party mode". His claim is that an attitude of generosity and enjoyment, treating friends well, not counting small costs, invites grace; stinginess over trivial amounts is, in his view, the sign of a poor Neptune. 01:18:55
  • Pluto's fix: simple devotion. He suggests bowing toward the south, or lighting a lamp there, as a minimal practice, grouping Pluto with a mythological role as the god of justice/dharma (Dharmaraj). 01:23:08
  • He adds that chanting, kirtan or devotional singing is, in his claim, a way of keeping all three of these forces (Indra, Neptune, Pluto) in balance at once. 01:23:08
  • He contrasts how much ease a small child enjoys, delighting in simple play, with how little ease an adult allows themselves, and calls this difference in attitude the real gap in "party mode", not a difference in actual circumstances. 01:19:25
  • Extreme stinginess over very small sums, in his telling, is itself the clearest sign of a weak Neptune, regardless of a person's actual wealth. 01:19:57
  • Being overly selective or stingy about who or what you will accept, he warns, blocks Neptune; staying open-handed, even if it looks careless to others, is in his claim what keeps work and opportunity moving. 01:20:28
  • He claims people born before 1960 systematically carry a weaker Indra than people today, reasoning that extreme personal wealth used to be far rarer in that generation. 01:14:45
  • He treats the three figures as converted forms of the same underlying force: the giver of grace (Varuna/Neptune), the giver of pleasure (Indra), and the one who ultimately "settles the account" through death or dharma (Pluto/Dharmaraj). 01:22:37

11. Questions: guru, surrender and stones as damage control 01:24:42

Can witness mind itself be a kind of hypnosis? 01:23:40

Yes, he says: witness mind (sakshi bhav) is itself a form of conscious "hypnosis" you choose, where you notice your own anger or confusion as it happens. His example: admitting "I don't know this, please tell me" instead of staying embarrassed is sakshi bhav in action, since shame has dropped away. 01:24:10

Do I need a guru, and does Jiddu Krishnamurti's "follow no one" advice contradict that? 01:29:53

His answer: there is no single rule for everyone. Some people need a guru who works through a kind of hypnosis/devotion, some don't, and different teachers suit different people. He says a teacher who tells people to reject all gurus is also, in effect, a guru for the people that approach suits. 12. A worked chart: how the same dosha plays out 01:58:53

The class closes with the speaker reading a live chart as a teaching example. It is a real consultation, so these notes summarise only the method he demonstrates (how a retrograde or combust planet changes its reading, how a house's ruler sitting in another house redirects its results) and compress the many repeated gemstone and thread remedies into one line each. Specific personal predictions about one individual's marriage, fertility or finances are the speaker's own chart reading, not a general rule, and are not a professional opinion on anyone's real situation.

  • Retrograde and combust planets. A planet very close to the Sun is "combust" (ast) and its effect is suppressed; a retrograde planet's influence is delayed or reversed rather than expressed directly. His rule: both combust and retrograde placements respond better to a gemstone than a placement that is neither. 02:18:12
  • A house ruler's seat decides the house's story. He repeatedly traces a house's outcome (marriage, career, father's relationship) by locating that house's ruling planet elsewhere in the chart and reading its strength and company there, rather than only looking at the house itself. 02:02:34
  • He reads a Jupiter sitting alongside another planet as never allowing either friendship or conflict to fully resolve, since Jupiter (in his framework) blocks both outcomes equally. 01:59:57
  • Marital strain, in-law interference and difficulty divorcing are, in his reading of this chart, tied to Saturn's placement over the relationship house, which he says can block a clean separation even when the marriage is unhappy. 02:04:41
