34. Munna's weapon: old memories (the Mangeram story)

  • "In this period Munna plays a game for ego. He has a very strong weapon. Today you are talking beautifully with your friend. My ego was broken 20 years ago by being beaten up by Mangeram. And I have kept those memories of the Mangeram beating safe. And those memories, even 20 years later, are spoiling my day."

The command technique for old memories

  1. "Sit comfortably. Do you get old memories? Sit comfortably and meditate. 'This one is my memory, okay? You stay in the past.'"
  2. "Remember: 'you were 25, maybe 26, maybe 30. This fight happened with me.' And whenever that comes: 'my father beat me in childhood, I feel like beating my father too.' So command those memories: 'you stay in the past where you are. Do not come to me.' Finished. Freeze it right there."
  3. "Your memories come to you to trouble you. They will end."
  • Question: What about good and bad memories? Answer: "There is no need for the good ones either. They will torment you. Good memories show dreams. If not fulfilled, those too (hurt). When you meet someone old, old things begin. If you meet good people, it is just fun, so keep the good ones. That is, keep only the good memories, by your own choice. Your old memories also give you frightening dreams for the future. Command them: 'this memory, you stay behind.'"

39. Story: the gatekeeper sadhu and the jamun fruit

"A very beautiful story, everyone must have heard the old one."

  1. There was a royal palace. The king gave the palace to disciple sadhus. The sadhus were appointed. There was one sadhu, very old, still unlettered: he could not read, could not write, could do nothing. So they put him at the gate: "Brother, you stand at the gate. You do nothing. You open the door. You listen to those who come and go, give water, give roti. If it is the rainy season give shelter. What else is there to do?"
  2. "What do we do with such a big palace?" They made it a math (monastery). Now he only opened the gate. Slowly his age grew. He knew nothing. Very simple, very beautiful behaviour, great grace of God. Not a trace of ego, no greed, nothing.
  3. With time, time changes. The math was completely full, 50 people came in it. The main priest came, the mahant came. One day a village man came to him and said: "I brought these jamuns (black plums) for you, brother." (The gatekeeper.)
  4. The gatekeeper said: "Oh, such beautiful jamuns! We do not get to eat these. We just eat whatever dal-bhat we get in alms. Seeing these jamuns, it feels like just looking is enough. But where am I worthy of this?" The farmer: "No, you people, you have seated me inside many times in the rain, given me water, taken care of me. You are the one who opens the door. What is there without you? Keep them." "No, I am not worthy. I will give them to the head." "If you want to give them away, friend, no. I did not bring them for the head, I brought them for you. I will not give to the head."
  5. "Friend, seeing your love I truly feel like keeping. I do not understand, I am not worthy, how do I eat, friend, you brought such beautiful jamuns." The farmer left. Now he sat four hours, looking at the jamuns, gazing and gazing. "Such fine jamuns, how do I eat them alone? I will do this: I will give them to the mukhiya ji, he will distribute, and I will take one. Not the way, he would not agree."
  6. He took them to the mukhiya ji (head). "Here, ji, such jamuns have come." "Friend, these are truly wonderful. Who gave them?" "That farmer, friend, wonderful." "Oh, it looks like nectar. We never even get to see these. Did you see them?" "I have been looking at them for two hours." The head said: "Great fun." He left them and went away.
  7. Now the mukhiya did the same. "You are very dear, friend. Where did you bring them from? They do not grow here. Where from, did someone bring them from far? We have built a math in this royal palace. But we are paupers."
  8. The mukhiya remembered a child who was sick. For many days he could not eat or drink. He took them to that child and said: "Son, you eat them, my child. Very fine jamuns have come. Here we do not get anything to eat." The boy looked: "Guruji, this is wonderful, this is a new fruit. I have not seen this, in fact you have seen and heard of, but I did not even know such fruits exist." He kept them and said "I will eat these."
  9. Now the head cook used to come to give him food. He came to him and said: "Here, today I have made good soup for you." With great difficulty, slowly, every day he fed him two spoons. The boy used to say "no, I will not eat, I am sick", but the cook kept stuffing him. He came again. Seeing him, tears came to the sick boy's eyes. "How much he loves me. I will give these to him. What he did in my service, even my mother could not. I will give them to him. He is the real one who acts (haraktdar). I have seen, I understand."
  10. He gave them to the head cook. The cook placed them in the kitchen. "Friend, how much love this boy has. He could have eaten them, and he is hungry, for how many days has he not got food like this. How much love is in his heart, how much respect for me. I feel like (eating) these jamuns, but no problem." He looked at the jamuns and was very moved. "I feel sorrow seeing this, that he thought me worthy." He said: "But whatever I am here, the grace is of our chief Mahant Maharaj, because of whom I am here. Otherwise where would I get such love? Whoever is the rightful owner of this." He took the jamuns to the chief Mahant and put them at his feet.
  11. He said: "Ji, you are the doer of everything here. On your strength we live, learn, take knowledge, eat and drink. These I give to you." He (the Mahant) saw: "Wow, wonderful. Where are they coming from, travelling?" "Wonderful. In the children's minds there is so much love, and so much devotion toward me. But what kind of guru am I? Shame on me if I should become a guru. I am the head, they understand everything about me, and I alone eat the jamuns?" He thought all night. Then he thought: "How did I get my standing? One day I too came here for the first time as a disciple, and for me the first time the gate was opened by that gatekeeper, because of whom I came here and learnt something and became something. My real guru is him."
  12. In the morning he got up and called the gatekeeper and gave him the jamuns. Now the gatekeeper saw: "What a journey this has been! What a look the jamuns have! They are not content anywhere. Think how lovingly the giver gave them. The jamuns made their own house, went around everywhere, and reached where they had to reach."
  • The speaker's point: "If there is truly love in your mind, then in every case what is yours comes to you. And where is love? Not having ego. Love is naturally there. It is there, it is the eternal truth (shashwat satya). As soon as your eyes open, it is seen by itself, breath goes by itself. Ego came, love went, and when love goes, 'more, more' starts."
