  2. The Moon is the fastest, most restless planet. It changes sign every two and a quarter days and is said to govern the mind, memory and the body's water.

1. Pride, water, and why flexibility matters 00:00:01

The class opens with a parable: a proud man boasts that his household could not survive a day without him, so a saint sends him away for three months. His family manages fine, helped along by a wealthy patron, and when the man sneaks back at night his own children do not recognise him and threaten to beat him off as a ghost. He returns to the saint in tears, his pride broken. 00:00:01

The claim

Pride (ghamand) is the belief that the world runs only because of you, whether that is a household, a business or a team at work. 00:01:33

His explanation

The body is roughly 72 percent water, and water is naturally flexible. Pride makes the neck stiffen and the body rigid; when that pride finally breaks, the trapped water is released as tears. 00:04:40

What he recommends

Stay flexible rather than rigid: let life adjust you rather than fighting to control everything yourself. 00:04:40

  • His comparison of older and younger generations: elders of earlier decades stayed limber well past 60 or 80, while today's young adults, in his view, are more "brittle" from eating very hot, heavily processed fast food, which he claims dries out the body's fluid content. 00:04:40 He points to post-tandoori thirst as evidence the body is signalling for water it has just lost. 00:05:11
  • He treats a lit, flowing, unfrozen quality in a person's temperament as healthy, versus a stiff, "set like curd" mentality that insists on rigid right-and-wrong rules for eating, sleeping and timing. 00:05:11 A body that keeps this fluidity, he claims, resists the usual brittleness of ageing bones. 00:05:43
  • He sides with doctors who tell anxious patients that stress itself, not diet, causes more damage than any food choice, since most of what people worry about (an unsold house, an unmarried child) resolves with time regardless. 00:04:40
  • Water, he says, is the single most important substance the universe searches for, present (by his account) on the Moon, under the surface of Mars, among some moons of Neptune, and trapped inside Pluto. 00:05:43

The claim that fast food specifically dries out the body's "water element," and the broader comparison between older and younger generations' physical resilience, are the speaker's own framework, not medical fact. These notes do not endorse changing diet or lifestyle on this basis.

  • Thought (via the Moon) is called the fastest-moving force in life, while the slowest is said to be Pluto (Yamraj, the force of death), who is described as sending Saturn for a "temporary" blow first and only arriving in person, quietly, when the body's fluids genuinely stop moving. 00:08:20

  • The Moon placed in each of the 12 houses of a chart is said to set a person's basic temperament: their stubbornness, which relationships they clash in, how money flows for them, and whether they tend to deceive themselves or others. 00:09:55 He adds that the Moon's own strongest trait is memory. 00:10:57

Take a small glass dish of water, place a written word (he gives examples like a name, or even an insult) underneath it, and freeze it. His claim is that the ice will somehow take on the "shape" of whatever was written underneath, and that this is why a chance meeting with someone you once fought with can instantly bring back the old hostility: the body's water is said to hold an imprint of it. 00:11:27

  • He links moksha (liberation) to becoming free of the Moon's grip on old memories and reactions: total freedom from caring whether you are praised or blamed, winning or losing. 00:13:01
  • His description of a settled, "pure" Moon: once a person learns to live with themselves rather than needing to change others, the Moon keeps moving through the chart as it always does, but the person watches it with detached awareness (sakshi bhav) instead of being swept along by anger, greed or low mood as they pass. 00:13:32 He lists anger, tension, greed and depression as the passing weather he means. 00:14:03

3. Manifestation, Schrodinger's cat, and "whatever happens is for the best" 00:15:07

A long tangent connects the Moon to manifestation. His claim: the universe behaves like a mirror, returning whatever feeling a person sends outward, and this is where he brings in two thought experiments from physics to argue his case. 00:15:07

The thought experiment

Schrodinger's cat: sealed in a box with a vial that may or may not have released poison, the cat is said to be neither dead nor alive until the box is opened and observed. 00:16:40

His reading of it

Before you decide what will happen, "anything can happen." The moment you fix your attention on one outcome (he calls this "vision," or one-pointed attention), that is what tends to show up. 00:17:43

His practical move

Say "I don't care either way, and whatever happens will be for the good" rather than fearing a specific bad outcome. This keeps the mind from "activating" the worst-case version through fear and worry. 00:17:12

  • His example of the opposite: repeating "we're finished, this deal is over" out of sheer worry is treated as giving the cosmos a direct instruction, which he claims the Moon (as the mind's agent) will carry out. 00:17:12 He is blunt about it: the cosmos, in his telling, simply executes whatever command it is given. 00:17:12
  • His account of a wish reaching the cosmos. A thought ("I want 100 crore") becomes an electrical signal in the body, becomes an electromagnetic wave, and is said to be captured first by the Moon and relayed outward; whether it actually returns depends on whether the person's own inner state (he says, their own residual sense of "but I'm broke") matches or contradicts the wish. 00:18:48 If the inner state still says "I am not that person," he claims the wish simply cannot land. 00:19:52
  • He returns to the same observer theme later using the Young double-slit experiment: a stream of electrons fired at a screen with two slits behaves like a spread-out wave (going through both slits at once) until a camera is added to observe it, at which point each electron behaves like a single particle landing in one place. His reading: your own inner awareness ("the water inside you") already "knows" whether it is being watched, which he offers as evidence for why focused attention changes outcomes. 01:35:12
  • His blunter formulation of detachment: deciding "I don't care either way" is said to active a kind of 50-50 split, so that even a bad outcome, in his claim, cannot exceed roughly half-strength, because no stronger emotional charge was fed into it. 01:34:42

The speaker's application of Schrodinger's cat and the double-slit experiment to personal wishes and "manifestation" is his own interpretation of these physics thought experiments, not an established scientific claim, and is not how physicists use them. These notes report it as his belief only.

Signs at home

Water leakage or seepage anywhere in the house: a dripping tap, a damp patch, standing water. He treats this as a direct, practical sign of a disturbed Moon, separate from any chart reading. 00:31:59

A disturbed Moon is said to show up as a foggy mind and a dulled sense of romance, playfulness and simple enjoyment. 00:31:59
