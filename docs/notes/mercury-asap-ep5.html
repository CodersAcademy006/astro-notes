The talk in brief

  1. Mercury is a pipe, not a person. It carries truth, lies, poison or nectar through your throat without judgment. What matters is only that the word lands clearly.
  2. It is a neuter planet. Not male, not female, not good, not bad. In the speaker's own framework this is why it has no fixed loyalty of its own.
  3. Mercury and Rahu exalt each other. Mercury does best in Rahu's house (Virgo), and Rahu does best in Mercury's house (Gemini).
  4. Keep the Mercury-Venus scale level. Mercury earns, Venus enjoys. Tip too far either way and the other pan empties out.
  5. Mercury governs teeth, nerves, joints and the small intestine. A disturbed Mercury also shows up as paperwork trouble, stammering and muddled speech.
  6. Mercury in the 6th or 12th house is a business warning. The speaker calls this combination, in any sign, a reason to stay out of business, lending and speculation.
  7. An emerald is Mercury's main gemstone. Green cloth, whole green moong dal and caring for a goat or parrot are his everyday remedies, alongside alum for the evil eye.

2. The birth story: Moon, Tara and why Mercury is green 00:10:59

A traditional story, told as the speaker tells it, for why Mercury carries no fixed loyalty and why its colour is green.

Mercury travels so closely alongside the Sun that the speaker also calls the Sun its adoptive father in this telling. 00:11:29

Jupiter (Brihaspati), king of the gods' teachers, is married to Tara. The Moon, restless and fickle by nature (the speaker notes it changes zodiac sign every two and a half to three days 00:11:59), falls for Tara, and a child is born of that union: Mercury. On realising Jupiter is not his biological father, Mercury resents both parents, the Moon for an "illegitimate" birth and Jupiter for not truly being his father. Jupiter's response, in the story, is to forgive and embrace him as his own regardless: "I am older, I am his guardian, whatever my wife did happened, I love you anyway." 00:12:29

Mercury's answer is different: "I don't care, I will use my own intellect; this is why the world keeps fighting, everyone insists their own god, their own faith is bigger." The speaker's point: without Jupiter's wisdom tempering it, raw intellect becomes constant one-upmanship and conflict, inside a single household as much as between nations. Four people in one home can each want a different vegetable for dinner because each is "using their own intellect" and no one will agree with anyone else. 00:13:01

  • The sky's blue is really a trick of distance (we cannot see far enough into the vacuum for it to look black up close), and the speaker calls everything in that endless blue "Rahu." Whatever fills the near, bounded space within that blue, like the inside of a cup, he calls Mercury. 00:04:43

  • Jupiter's colour is yellow. Rahu's is blue. Mixed through the Moon's water (moisture, saliva, the fluid element in the body) the two make green, which the speaker gives as the real reason Mercury's colour is green and why green notes, green vegetation and green cloth are tied to this planet. 00:14:33
  • A chandal yoga (an inauspicious pairing) between Jupiter and Rahu is traditionally seen as a problem, but the speaker reframes it: when Jupiter's wisdom and an awakened (witness-minded) Rahu combine well, "the chandal becomes a guru," and what comes out of your mouth is nectar rather than poison. His own reframing of a traditionally inauspicious yoga; a personal, not classical, reading. 00:15:35
  • Money follows this logic too: if your Mercury is good, money comes, but money itself does not care how it was earned, "Lakshmi makes no distinction either," in the speaker's words. His own view on how money is earned and judged; not a claim that any method of earning is endorsed. 00:07:52
  • What actually brings money, in his account, is not cleverness alone but firm resolve, "grab Krishna's finger," meaning commit fully to a course of action rather than earning through sheer intellect. 00:17:09

5. The balance scale: Mercury and Venus 00:41:48

The speaker's central image for the rest of the class: life runs on two pans of one scale, Mercury (intellect, trade, the capacity to earn) on one side and Venus (comfort, enjoyment, the capacity to enjoy what is earned) on the other. Tip one pan too far and the other falls.

Problem

A huge house that is never actually lived in, constant travel so the owner never enjoys their own comfort, or a very intelligent person who ends up working to make someone else rich rather than enjoying their own skill. 00:42:18

Cause

Mercury (earning, know-how) has run far ahead of Venus (the capacity to actually enjoy what is earned), or the reverse: comfort without the sense to manage it. 00:43:19

The speaker's framing

Learn to feel satisfied with what you actually experience rather than what you accumulate. In his words, wealth taken into the next life is "nothing, seven is all they say you carry," so the measure that matters is how much of what you have was actually enjoyed. His own view on satisfaction and wealth; not financial advice. 00:43:50
